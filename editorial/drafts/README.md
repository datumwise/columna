## the_yes_machine_problem_v2.md

Preserved verbatim; not edited, not reformatted, not retitled.

**What v2 changed** (per the covering ruling): it now names the real progress already happening —
governed semantic models, intermediate representations, deterministic execution, provenance, tests,
constrained interfaces — and then sharpens the remaining question to **"what establishes the
machine's right to say yes?"**

**What it deliberately is not, and must not become:** a Veezoo comparison, an Anthropic comparison,
or any competitor-relative piece. No competitor names, no comparative claims, no datumwise
vocabulary. It is meant to stay personal, approachable, non-argumentative, broadly shareable, and
readable by someone who has never heard of us — a **Door into deeper exploration**, not a summary of
the corpus. (Checked on filing: the draft names no competitor and uses no datumwise term of art.)

**Its Door status is editorial and lives nowhere but the surface that renders it** — ruled 2026-08-27:
Door status must not be added to `registry/sources/sources.json`, there is no `door` flag, no Door
authority class, and no Door corpus layer. See `specs/doors_recon_and_proposal_v0_1.md`.

**Open, deliberately:** the canonical external URL. When the LinkedIn Article is published it becomes
an **external Door**, marked as an external crossing in the site's existing convention, verified once
at integration time. No onsite duplicate is to be created merely to make the Door uniform — the
heterogeneity is intentional, because a Door's function is passage, not artifact normalisation.
Whether the article ever earns an onsite home is a later decision and explicitly not being made now.
