Abstract

A value becomes analytical data when it is bound to a governed analytical point. The Theory of Data describes the objects and laws needed to identify such data independently of its physical representation. A universe establishes which root points exist. An anchor partitions those points into analytical locations. A measure family supplies a governed analytical identity, and a measure is that family at one current anchor, written F@AF@A. Analytical lineage records how family identity is constituted.

Version 7.1 retains Version 7.0’s constructive family formation and sufficient-state family relations, and clarifies their continuation, evidence, and reuse obligations. A family law specifies its asserted target, constitutive inputs, admitted anchors and movements, participation, semantic value domains, sufficient-state constructions, and exceptional cases. Sufficient state remains a relationship among ordinary measure families: SUM and COUNT can construct MEAN; a set-valued family can construct exact distinct count. Each admitted basis must correctly construct the independently specified target on its own domain. Sufficiency does not imply necessity, equal material availability, or interchangeability for every later operation.

A family law may depend on a governed order of analytical points while remaining invariant to evidence enumeration. The admitted multidimensional construction specifies complete point orders within constituent dimensions and precedence among those dimensions. These induce an order on the existing analytical point space, without creating absent coordinate combinations. FIRST and LAST use ordinary witness-valued families whose finite continuation satisfies the same commutative-state coherence theorem as other self-sufficient families. A further proposition establishes the exact compression from a retained point–value family to the extremum witness.

The paper separates contextual formation from continuation, mathematical coherence from current evidence adequacy, and a stored answer from retained reusable state. A coherent target may be established even when a particular sufficient-state basis or staged materialization is unavailable. Lossy state does not certify its own input-consistency premises. The foundation includes a compact law catalog and an index to the inherited statistical extensions, preserved in an accompanying reference supplement under their declared contracts. It states the boundaries with semantic types, analytical requests, assurance, physical realization, and statistical inference. Its claim is conditional and focused: given governed definitions and adequate evidence, analytical identity, derivability, consistency, and reuse can be made explicit and independently adjudicable.

1. The foundation and its scope

The Theory of Data begins with a simple observation:

Data is always something about something else.

The value 42 alone does not identify an analytical datum. Age = 42 at John binds a typed value to a point it describes. A more elaborate record, such as Revenue of 420 for Customer C17 in July, raises the same questions: which customer and period, in which analytical universe, under which Revenue definition, and with what evidence?

These questions are not answered by the existence of a row. A table can carry a value without establishing the quantity it represents. A join can repeat a valid amount without creating a finer measure of that amount. A stored average can be correct at its original location while lacking the information required for exact continuation. Two lawful computations can return equal numbers while describing different quantities.

The central analytical reference is

measure=measure family@anchor. \boxed{\text{measure}=\text{measure family}@\text{anchor}.}

A family answers what quantity is being claimed. Its current anchor answers where that quantity lives. The family law determines which other measures of that family can be established, under what formation and participation, and from what sufficient state. A physical table, cached object, query expression, or output label does not establish those facts merely by existing.

1.1 What the theory governs

ToD governs analytical data: its universe, geometry, identity, formation, derivability, sufficient state, lineage, and consistency. It is not a theory of every object that might participate in analysis. It does not determine a business definition’s wisdom, the empirical truth of an observation, the validity of a statistical inference, or whether an established result should authorize a business action.

The ontology is category-level. A Manifold can constitute an enterprise’s governed analytical objects under ToD; its local definitions do not define the theory itself. A semantic type specification supplies the value capabilities those laws require. A request language articulates a requested result. An implementation realizes it. These authorities interact without becoming interchangeable. [1, 2]

The governing editorial rule remains:

Keep a distinction in the foundation when it changes analytical identity, derivability, consistency, or the validity of those claims. Otherwise treat it as a contract, consequence, or application of the core.

This scope keeps the common analytical object visible while allowing precise contracts for difficult cases.

1.2 From datum to the formal dependency

The intellectual starting point is the datum: a value must be about something. The formal account then steps back to establish the point itself. An existence law determines the root-point domain; anchors partition it; families and their measures can then be constituted over those locations.

The conceptual dependency is:

universe existence
    → analytical points and anchor geometry
    → families, measures, and constitutive formation
    → sufficient-state constructions and lawful continuation
    → identity-relative consistency and justified reuse

This is not an execution pipeline. A proof or a request may use a different order of reasoning. In particular, current evidence need establish only the dependencies required by the target law, not every possible intermediate object.

1.3 What Version 7.1 changes

The published Version 7.0, dated 3 September 2026, is the immediate predecessor of this revision. It already established canonical family-forming expressions, constitutive inner anchors, sufficient-state relations among ordinary measure families, basis adequacy, and well-founded composite construction. It admitted self-sufficient associative and commutative continuation as a semigroup on nonempty supported fibers or as a monoid where an identity exists. It also distinguished analytical materializability from physical storage and kept approximation in the certificate/materialization layer when the semantic target remained fixed. These are inherited commitments, not new results of Version 7.1. [2]

Version 7.1 changes the categorical exclusion of analytical-point-order-dependent families. A family law can depend on a complete governed order among analytical points while its sufficient-state combination remains invariant to evidence enumeration. FIRST and LAST demonstrate that possibility through ordinary witness-valued families and the common finite coherence theorem. Their constitutive order does not change the underlying anchor’s partition identity.

This correction is narrower than a return to every Version 6.1 state-law class. It does not admit enumeration-sensitive combination or a family with no independently justified compositional basis merely because the operation is useful or was historically called a family. The well-founded sufficient-state requirement remains. The new order construction specifies complete constituent point orders and dimension precedence; it supplies no physical tie-breaking permission.

The revision also distinguishes contextual formation from subsequent continuation and separates basis correctness, present evidence adequacy, and materialized reuse. The reviewed witness-compression proposition and partial-evidence examples make those obligations harder to misread. They refine the Version 7.0 architecture rather than replacing its ontology.

The inherited statistical catalog is preserved in an accompanying reference supplement at its published level of conditional contract completion; §11.6 provides the index. Relocating those entries changes their presentation, not their analytical standing. This manuscript does not newly prove every catalog entry or claim current product implementation. Section 13 states the proof and validation scope. Appendix C records continuity from Versions 6.1 and 7.0 and the explicit changes made here.

1.5 Inherited governance locations

Version 7.1 retains the four governance locations made explicit in Version 6.1 and inherited by Version 7.0. They classify information by what it changes, not by which software object stores it. [1, 2]

Location Governing question
Family identity What analytical quantity, formation, participation, and declared family law are being specified?
Edge validity Does this particular derivation faithfully realize the already-declared identity?
Certificate / materialization What exactness, approximation, evidence, retained capability, or realization standing does this representation carry while the semantic target remains fixed?
Metadata What descriptive or operational information accompanies the object without changing the preceding three?

A constitutive inner anchor or selected analytical order belongs to family identity when changing it changes the quantity or family law. Evidence that a particular route preserves that order belongs to edge validity. Retaining a point witness rather than only its scalar projection changes materialized capability, not the scalar family’s identity. An error certificate for an approximate realization of an unchanged target likewise does not, by itself, create another target family.

Participation and coverage illustrate why the governing question matters. Changing which contributions define a quantity can change family identity. Checking that a particular realization has the coverage required by that unchanged definition is an edge- or evidence-validity question. The same subject can therefore occur at different governance locations without the locations becoming interchangeable.

Structural conformance and institutional authority are also distinct. ToD specifies the declarations and well-formedness obligations a selected publication must satisfy; the surrounding governance process selects which conforming publication is authoritative. Section 12.3 preserves this boundary without prescribing a publication schema.

2. Geometry, identity, and standing

2.1 Universes and anchors

2.1.1 Point existence and partitions

A universe UU has a governed root-point domain ΩU\Omega_U under an existence law λU\lambda_U. The law answers what makes a root point exist in this analytical world. Under an occurrence-based law, a point may exist because an event occurred. Under a declared or generated law, an operating-calendar store-day can exist independently of a sales observation. Event-like and spine-like domains are forms of the existence law, not additional top-level ontological kinds. [1, 2]

The root is relative to the declared analytical world. It need not be the finest description possible in physical reality. A daily state can be primitive in one universe while another universe models events from which that state might be constructed. Primitive dimensions and analytical inputs are governed starting facts in that universe; the theory does not infer them from physical naming conventions.

The root points determine the root anchor

RU={{ω}:ω∈ΩU}. R_U=\big\{\{\omega\}:\omega\in\Omega_U\big\}.

An anchor AA is a governed partition of ΩU\Omega_U. Its points are nonempty blocks, with

a∩a′=⌀(a≠a′),⋃a∈Aa=ΩU. a\cap a'=\varnothing\quad(a\ne a'), \qquad \bigcup_{a\in A}a=\Omega_U.

Thus an anchor is a complete analytical organization of the governed universe, not an arbitrary grouping expression or list of columns.

2.1.2 Refinement and incomparable anchors

For anchors of the same universe, write B≽AB\succeq A when every BB-point lies in exactly one AA-point. This gives the unique projection

πB→A:B→A. \pi_{B\to A}:B\to A.

Write B≻AB\succ A when refinement is strict. Equality permits identity movement; it is not a strictly contracting reducer. For compatible refinements, projection composition gives

πI→A=πB→A∘πI→Bwhen I≽B≽A. \pi_{I\to A}=\pi_{B\to A}\circ\pi_{I\to B} \quad\text{when }I\succeq B\succeq A.

Refinement is a partial order. Two anchors can both be governed without either refining the other. Calendar Week and Calendar Month are familiar examples: Day can refine each, while a week can cross a month boundary. A valid weekly materialization does not thereby establish exact calendar-month values. A physical join does not supply a missing partition projection.

2.1.3 Dimensions and sparse compound anchors

A dimension gives a conventional governed name to a partition. Hierarchies describe refinement among such partitions; a dimension need not belong to one universal hierarchy. The fact that two dimension values can be joined is distinct from the claim that one partition refines another.

A compound anchor is a governed common refinement. For constituent partitions A1,…,AnA_1,\ldots,A_n, its points are the nonempty intersections of their blocks over the declared universe. When those constituents jointly determine anchor SS, their projections give an injection

q:S↪∏i=1nAi. q:S\hookrightarrow\prod_{i=1}^{n}A_i.

Its image need not be the full Cartesian product. The universe law determines which root points exist; refinement organizes them. Neither an order nor a grouping operation creates an unoccupied coordinate combination. The constituent description need not be a uniquely discoverable factorization, and its dimensions need not be statistically independent.

An overlapping relationship does not automatically induce a partition or authorize repeated contribution. Assignment, allocation, membership universes, and full-touch expansion require governed structural constructions; Appendix D gives the inherited distinction. These constructions do not weaken the anchor law.

The anchor expression and the partition it denotes should also remain distinct. Two expressions that happen to group current records identically are not thereby analytically equivalent. Equivalence comes from the governing structure, not an accident of current data. [1, 2]

2.2 Datum, family identity, and the current anchor

A datum is one governed typed value at one analytical point. Informally, for point aa and value vv, write v@av@a. The point, value domain, and quantity being asserted are part of the analytical account. A physical key may represent the point; it does not define the point’s analytical standing merely by being unique in a table.

A measure family is a governed analytical identity with determinate formation and admitted measures under its law. The full contract is stated in §4. A measure is that family at one current anchor:

F@A. \boxed{F@A.}

A family may be named directly, as with a governed revenue, or denoted through an admitted canonical construction, such as mean(revenue@order). The canonical expression exposes identity-bearing choices; its presence in text does not by itself prove admission.

A canonical name resolves to one family identity within a governed namespace and version. The immutable family identity is not the same object as its human-readable name. A family may have aliases, but a resolved analytical reference must be unambiguous. Two distinct active identities cannot be hidden under one ambiguous canonical reference.

Constitutive anchors, operands, order definitions, participation conventions, and other meaning-bearing parameters remain inside FF. They do not become additional current anchors of F@AF@A. Thus

mean(revenue@order)@region mean(revenue@order)@region

has a constitutive Order anchor and one current Region anchor. Replacing Order by Customer can change the family even when the result is displayed at the same Region location.

The ex-ante identity-bearing declaration may be canonicalized as a semantic signature Σ(F)\Sigma(F), to which an immutable family_id is bound. Its content includes the governed universe, canonical family form, constitutive parents and anchors, identity-bearing law parameters and contracts, and constitutive analytical lineage (§3.7). The choice of a lawful derivation path or proof method for an already specified target is not identity-bearing; meaning-bearing formation choices remain part of its constitution. A digest is one possible implementation, not a required mechanism. Following Version 7.0, the signature is not an additional ontological kind.

family_id≠canonical_name. \boxed{family\_id\ne canonical\_name.}

Equal present values do not establish equal analytical identities. Different physical representations can establish the same analytical object. Identity is resolved before the relevant consistency comparison is made.

Throughout the proofs, one coherent analytical instance and its governing context are fixed. The notation F@AF@A is not a complete physical cache key. Different snapshots, populations, or unresolved restrictions cannot be substituted merely because their compact family-and-anchor labels match. [1, 2]

2.3 Existence, eligibility, and value support

At an established anchor, a family may have eligible points EF,A⊆AE_{F,A}\subseteq A and supported points SF,A⊆EF,AS_{F,A}\subseteq E_{F,A}. Its supported value map is

mF,A:SF,A→VF. m_{F,A}:S_{F,A}\to V_F.

A point can be absent from the universe, present but ineligible for a quantity, eligible but unsupported, or supported with value zero. These are different analytical claims even when a carrier represents several of them with row absence or a null.

Evidence about existence can itself be insufficient. An existing root point’s placement under a particular anchor can also be unsupported. Eligibility may be unresolved rather than established or denied. These are dependencies to preserve, not instructions to create one universal standing enum.

For example, accepted evidence that a transaction record was lost can establish that the transaction occurred while failing to establish its Revenue or Day coordinate. Revenue recovered from a separate source can support a coarser total while Day-dependent claims remain unsupported. Conversely, no record and no evidence of occurrence does not by itself establish that an event did not occur.

Support does not silently shrink the universe or replace eligibility. A missing measure value is an eligible-but-unsupported value at an established analytical point; it is not an omitted anchor in a request, an invalid partition, or an unsupported existence claim. The evidence needed for a particular target is determined by that target’s law (§9).

Here SF,AS_{F,A} denotes an evidence-supported subset. By contrast, the standalone symbol SS used later denotes a constitutive input anchor. Earlier ordered-family drafts called it a support anchor. It is not a new anchor kind, and the two uses of support must not be conflated.

An anchor by itself does not determine a traversal order. A family requiring analytical-point order must bind to additional governed structure, developed in §7. Anchor-refinement order and the order of analytical points remain different relations. [2, 6]

3. Formation and continuation

3.1 Formation establishes what will be consumed

Formation determines the analytical input of a construction. It may use an already governed measure, several co-participating measures, an anchor-derived input such as point count, or a contextual expression whose meaning is itself established.

The formation contract identifies the operand, its constitutive anchor, eligibility, participation, type, and any context that affects its meaning. Multi-input formation must preserve the relationships the downstream law needs. For example, multiplying values and weights after reducing them separately does not generally reconstruct their original paired products. [2, 3, 4]

Formation can change family identity. Merely returning a value, saving an expression, or assigning an alias does not by itself perform that governed act.

3.2 Continuation preserves a particular family law

Continuation asks which measures of an already specified family can be established from its admitted sufficient-state measures. It has its own geometric and algebraic obligations.

The formation anchor remains constitutive. It must not be replaced by the current intermediate anchor merely because a staged computation is convenient. An average over order values remains an average over order values when state is staged through Month. Re-averaging monthly displayed means without their weights is another computation.

The same distinction applies to an ordered family. Its intermediate state retains the relevant original analytical-point witness. Continuing through another anchor does not silently restart LAST over that anchor’s labels.

3.3 Contextual formation does not defeat later coherence

Consider an established daily series

100,110,95,105. 100,\quad110,\quad95,\quad105.

Under an explicit predecessor rule, form one-day differences at the last three points:

10,−15,10. 10,\quad-15,\quad10.

The first day is outside this difference expression’s eligibility because the required predecessor does not exist in the declared example. The subsequent difference values are formed using the original sequence, before regrouping.

A separately governed family that sums these already-formed one-day differences has ordinary additive continuation. Its total is 55, and any partition-and-recombine path over those same contributions agrees.

The difference at one day used another day’s value. That contextual formation history does not invalidate the addition of the established differences. On the other hand, recomputing differences inside each later group can change which predecessor is used or omit a boundary difference. That is a change of formation, not another continuation path of the same family.

The example shows why locality of continuation cannot be strengthened into a requirement that all formation history be local to the current point’s projection fiber. It does not automatically give ordinary LAG, rolling, or cumulative syntax an independent continuation law.

3.4 The corrected locality statement

For the finite commutative-state continuation described in §6, the state at a target point is formed from the admitted finer states in its projection fiber. Those finer states must already have their governed meaning and support.

This continuation property does not prohibit context in the formation of those states. No general impossibility theorem for lag-, rank-, cumulative-, or rolling-derived families follows from it.

An ordinary focal expression still needs its own context and result-locus rules. A proposed family built from it must separately establish a family law. Neither automatic promotion nor categorical exclusion is justified by the function name.

A family constructed from a contextual expression is admitted only when its constitutive formation, participation, and empty or undefined cases are specified, an independently establishable sufficient-state basis correctly constructs its target, and its claimed continuations are coherent over the admitted anchors without changing that formation. This is the common family contract, not a second admission system.

An implementation may faithfully recompute the original constitutive values from adequate governed evidence. The restriction is on changing their analytical formation or context, not on computing them again.

3.5 Pointwise and tuple expressions

An anchorable expression can combine governed operands without itself acquiring durable family identity. For an admitted pointwise operation ⋆\star,

(E1⋆E2)@A=(E1@A)⋆(E2@A) (E_1\star E_2)@A=(E_1@A)\star(E_2@A)

where the operands can be lawfully co-established under the applicable universe, type, and co-participation contract. Division also requires the denominator and exceptional-case conditions of the expression. Equal coordinate spelling alone does not establish all these premises.

A tuple has the corresponding reading

(E1,E2)@A=(E1@A,E2@A) (E_1,E_2)@A=(E_1@A,E_2@A)

under the governed pairing contract. Its internal components do not become another analytical anchor. For covariance and correlation, the pair must be established while the operand relationship is available. Marginally supported operands do not by themselves establish joint participation.

Ordinary arithmetic such as revenue/orders can therefore be a lawful request-level expression. By itself, an anchorable expression has no family ID, independent family lineage, continuation law, or analytical materializability. Physical storage of a computed result remains possible; storage does not supply those analytical permissions. A later family-forming law may consume the expression, or a governed declaration may establish a family from it under the full contract. Neither an output alias nor the mere fact of computability mints a family.

The conservation rule is:

An operation cannot use a relationship that was neither retained nor reconstructed from governed evidence.

A weighted mean, for example, requires pointwise products at its constitutive anchor before separate reductions destroy the pairing. This is an instance of governed formation, not a special exception for one statistic. [2, 3, 4]

3.6 Operator geometry does not confer family authority

A mapper preserves its current anchor. A reducer contracts a strictly finer anchor to a coarser one within a universe. Schematic forms are

F@A→mapperG@A,F@B→reducerG@A,B≻A, F@A\xrightarrow{mapper}G@A, \qquad F@B\xrightarrow{reducer}G@A,\quad B\succ A,

when the outputs have governed family standing. A general expression can also produce a result without minting a family. Geometric shape and family admission are separate judgments.

An ordered FIRST/LAST construction can change anchor while satisfying a family law. An anchor-preserving contextual transformation can form inputs for a later family. Neither operation receives or loses family authority solely from an implementation classification.

Structural operations such as restriction, alignment, expansion, allocation, and universe passage have their own contracts. In particular, replication of a coarse value for participation in a finer expression does not establish a finer measure of the same family. An execution plan may implement these operations, but it cannot create their analytical permissions by performing them. [1, 4]

3.7 Analytical lineage records constitutive ancestry

A family-level lineage graph records how family identities are established. A directed edge from FF to GG records that the governed constitution of GG depends on FF. Several parents may participate in a multi-input construction. The relation is well-founded and rooted in the universe’s governed primitive inputs; it does not consist of every reversible operational conversion.

For example, forming mean(revenue@order) records Revenue at the constitutive Order anchor and the MEAN law. Forming a covariance records both operands and their governed paired formation. Constitutive anchors remain identity-bearing where changing them changes the quantity. A MAX formed from Order Revenue and a MAX formed after Revenue is established at Day are different constructions unless a governing equivalence proves otherwise.

Lineage is therefore within analytical governance. It answers what the family is and how that identity was constituted. Physical job lineage or column dependency can contribute evidence, but it is not a substitute for the analytical account. [1, 2]

Average Order Value is the inherited multi-parent example. If a governed law explicitly establishes the family average_order_value, then on its admitted domain

average_order_value@A=revenue@AorderCount@A. average\_order\_value@A=\frac{revenue@A}{orderCount@A}.

Its constitutive lineage names both parent identities and the synthesis law. The ordinary expression revenue/orders need not mint that family, but the established family is not prohibited merely because its constructor is a ratio. A matching Revenue and OrderCount basis can reconstruct it at each admitted anchor. With child states (100,1)(100,1) and (300,2)(300,2), the combined value is 400/3400/3, not the unweighted mean 125125 of the displayed child ratios. This is the same sufficient-state discipline, not a special ratio primitive. [1, 2]

3.8 Lineage and sufficient-state dependency answer different questions

The ancestry of mean(revenue@order) and its sufficient-state basis are not the same description:

Constitutive lineage:
    revenue at Order -- MEAN law --> mean(revenue@order)

One target construction at A:
    sum(revenue@order)@A   ─┐
                           ├─ governed constructor ─> mean(revenue@order)@A
    count(revenue@order)@A ─┘

The first description identifies the family and its formation. The second identifies measures that can construct that family’s measure at a target anchor. An alternative sufficient-state basis does not automatically change the family’s constitutive identity. Section 5 supplies the admission and agreement obligations for those bases.

3.9 Names, canonical construction, and succession

A governed single-valued default completion may allow a shorter reference to resolve to an already specified construction. It does not create identity or choose freely among different analytical meanings. revenue can resolve to an explicit sum-family construction only where identity and participation agree under the governed declaration.

If an identity-bearing definition changes, the result is a successor family identity. A later namespace version may retain a familiar local name while preserving the distinction between the old and new immutable identities. A mere label change need not change identity. Conversely, a change of constitutive order, participation, or formation cannot be concealed by retaining the old label.

A change of physical representation, retained basis, or execution route is not by itself family succession. A change to an identity-bearing target, formation, participation, or declared continuation law is. The sufficient-state alternatives of §5 therefore need not become different target families merely because they realize the same established law differently.

The rule is identity before comparison and versioned succession before silent redefinition. [1, 2]

4. One family-law contract

A measure family is a governed analytical identity with specified formation and admitted measures, together with sufficient-state constructions whose claimed continuations are coherent. In the finite continuation fragment developed here, reusable leaf-state families are grounded in commutative composition; composite families are constructed from such families through well-founded dependencies.

The contract has one authoritative account of the following facts. They are not separate ontological kinds or necessarily separate records.

Identity and ancestry Operand identities, constitutive anchors, law parameters, and well-founded constitutive analytical lineage sufficient to distinguish the family. Canonical names resolve this identity; they do not mint it (§§2.2, 3.9).

A declaration states a proposed definition. Human ratification establishes the declaration’s authority in its domain; it does not prove its mathematical laws. A proof establishes a conditional analytical statement; it does not certify that a particular source currently satisfies its premises. A backend computation establishes neither merely by returning a value.

4.1 Admitted anchors are part of the law

Write 𝒜F\mathcal A_F for the family law’s admitted anchors. This is notation for its defined analytical domain, not a proposed registry or new object. An edge contract ΓF(B→A)\Gamma_F(B\to A) states the applicable conditions for a proposed movement between admitted locations.

A geometrically available projection and a computable state operation do not by themselves put AA in 𝒜F\mathcal A_F. The family’s definition must license the quantity being claimed there. Conversely, a backend’s inability to realize an admitted movement does not remove that movement from analytical law.

The distinction matters for multidimensional data. A global LAST of one Customer–Day value, a LAST taken separately for each customer, and a SUM of those customer results are not automatically one family. Each must retain its actual law and ancestry.

4.2 Participation must survive staging

For a particular law instance, let DD be its governed participating domain at a constitutive anchor II. Participation is selected by the law and the resolved request; it is not automatically the set of surviving physical records or supported operand values.

A staged proof must combine the same intended contributions, with the same multiplicity and formation, as its direct counterpart. An intervening restriction, allocation, completion, or change of context requires its own justification. The word “staging” does not make such a change immaterial.

A request-local restriction need not create a new named family or universe. But any restriction that changes the analytical target must remain explicit in the resolved definition, request, or lineage as its law requires. It cannot disappear from equivalence and reuse checks.

5. Sufficient state remains ordinary analytical data

5.1 Target-relative bases

Retain the v7 notation

ℬ∈𝒮(F). \mathcal B\in\mathcal S(F).

A sufficient-state basis ℬ=(G1,…,Gm)\mathcal B=(G_1,\ldots,G_m) supplies the governed family measures required to construct F@AF@A wherever that basis is admitted. The tuple notation records input roles; it does not require a separate composite-state object.

For a governed constructor,

F@A=ϕF,ℬ(G1@A,…,Gm@A). F@A=\phi_{F,\mathcal B}(G_1@A,\ldots,G_m@A).

The equality applies on the constructor’s declared domain, under compatible participation and evidence. A basis may have a restricted domain of validity; an implementation cannot silently extrapolate it to every anchor or every missingness case. Sufficiency is not a claim that this basis is necessary, minimal, or recoverable from every other admitted basis.

The constructor is anchor-local: its required inputs are the already-established measures Gi@AG_i@A at the target anchor. If a chosen basis role is available only at finer BB, that particular plan must first establish Gi@AG_i@A under the law of GiG_i, recursively through its own basis where needed. This does not say that every possible basis or intermediate path must be available whenever the target has another adequate derivation (§9).

For a composite family, its basis must be establishable without first assuming the target value it is meant to construct. The identity map does not turn every family into its own sufficient state. A self-sufficient family must separately establish its continuation law. [2]

5.2 Self-sufficiency and source intake are different

A family is self-sufficient when its own supported measure values compose across admitted refinement by an associative and commutative continuation law. On nonempty supported fibers the law may form a commutative semigroup (VG,⊕G)(V_G,\oplus_G). Where an identity exists, it forms a commutative monoid (VG,⊕G,eG)(V_G,\oplus_G,e_G). MIN and MAX need not acquire an artificial identity or semantic Null in order to qualify. [2]

Self-sufficiency is a composition claim, not a coverage claim. The family’s declared target-coverage rule must also hold. An algebraic identity does not supply missing evidence or determine the value of an empty eligible fiber. Section 6 states the finite monoid theorem and preserves the nonempty semigroup case.

Continuing a supported family value is not the same act as feeding it back through the source-intake rule of a new construction.

For COUNT, two retained count states 3737 and 1212 can combine to 4949 under count-state continuation. Counting the two scalar values as new observations gives 22. The distinction is the analytical role of the inputs, not their integer type.

For MEAN, a displayed scalar generally loses the weight required for exact continuation. Its SUM and COUNT basis retains that information. No type label or materialized availability recovers it by default.

5.3 Alternative bases must agree

A family can have more than one admitted basis. For example, an exact finite mean can be constructed from matching SUM and COUNT measures, or from a retained multiset of the same participating values.

Admission first requires each basis to construct the target law on its own declared domain. Pairwise agreement alone is not enough: two incorrect constructions could agree, or their application domains might not overlap. Every family law must fix that target throughout its declared analytical domain through either an independent semantic specification over its governed analytical inputs and participating contributions or a nominated defining construction, including applicability and defined-result conditions (§4). The specification must not rely on the assertion that a candidate basis is sufficient. A nominated defining construction supplies the reference meaning; alternative constructions must establish equivalence to it.

A defining construction need not be a second algorithm distinct from every admitted basis. One construction may define the quantity and supply a basis, provided it is specified without assuming the target it defines and satisfies the independent-input and well-foundedness obligations (§§5.1 and 5.4). Its nomination does not establish the correctness of arbitrary implementations.

If ℬ\mathcal B and 𝒞\mathcal C both construct the same target under the same governing context, their soundness entails

ϕF,ℬ(ℬ@A)=ϕF,𝒞(𝒞@A) \phi_{F,\mathcal B}(\mathcal B@A) = \phi_{F,\mathcal C}(\mathcal C@A)

on their common admitted application domain. Here ℬ@A\mathcal B@A abbreviates its role-indexed family measures. Correctness includes the target’s applicability and defined-result conditions, not just equality of numbers after unsupported or out-of-domain cases have been omitted. One basis may have a narrower declared application domain; outside it the basis does not silently redefine the target.

Basis adequacy remains substantive. Within an admitted basis domain, two governed evidence configurations that agree on every required Gi@AG_i@A under the same relevant standing and contracts must determine the same F@AF@A, including every eligibility, support, and value distinction preserved by the target law. The constructor must compute that target. This is the Version 7.0 adequacy obligation, retained here rather than reduced to agreement between alternative formulas. [2]

For example, a count-only basis can be independently establishable, non-circular, and compositional yet fail to determine MEAN: two observations can have the same count and different means. No structural property of the dependency graph repairs that inadequate basis. The possibility of another proof of a target, or the current unavailability of one admitted basis, does not weaken this conditional obligation on the basis being used.

Determinism and path independence of each constructor do not prove target correctness. SUM and MAX can each be deterministic and coherent while denoting different quantities. A set is not interchangeable with a multiset for MEAN: the observations 0,0,60,0,6 have mean 22, while discarding multiplicity gives mean 33. Returning zero for a known-empty input also fails the nonempty scalar-MEAN contract, even if nonempty cases agree.

This obligation does not require all bases to contain the same information, to be mutually convertible, or to be materially available together. A retained multiset can preserve distinctions that SUM and COUNT discard while both remain valid bases for the same exact mean. Current evidence must establish the particular basis used, with matching participation, formation, and context across its roles. Individually supported inputs drawn from incompatible populations do not form an admitted basis merely because their types match.

Thus cross-basis agreement is an obligation of the existing sufficiency relation, not a new ontology or a claim that arbitrary formulas can be proved equivalent automatically.

5.4 Well-founded construction

Composite dependencies must terminate in independently established analytical inputs and self-sufficient families with established laws. A circular declaration that each family is sufficient for the other is not a construction proof. Operational conversions or alternative basis choices may be useful, but they do not justify circular constitutive identity.

This account retains v7’s rejection of a separate information-quotient ontology as the normative definition of sufficiency. It also retains compatible work on participation, conservation, pairing, and carrier adequacy. [2, 3]

5.5 A valid basis need not be available, and an available result need not retain a basis

Basis admission is a claim about analytical law. Availability is a claim about the current evidence. They have different scopes.

A retained complete point-value family and a winning-point/value family may both be admitted bases for scalar LAST. The complete retained family can be unavailable because some nonwinning values are missing, while the winning-point/value family is established from an adequately supported extremum. The target can then be constructed from the available basis; the absent basis has not become invalid.

Conversely, two materializations that display the same target value need not retain equivalent continuation information. A correct rendering of F@BF@B is not automatically an admissible substitute for the state required to construct F@AF@A. Later reuse must be checked against the later requested law. Sections 8.6, 9, and 10 give concrete cases. Section 10.7 states the materialization consequence: availability of a target must not be recorded as though it also established or retained its sufficient-state basis.

5.6 Ordinary examples of the same relation

For a fixed constitutive anchor and matched participation, one admitted MEAN basis is

{sum(x@I),count(x@I)}∈𝒮(mean(x@I)), \{sum(x@I),count(x@I)\}\in\mathcal S(mean(x@I)),

with the nonempty constructor

mean(x@I)@A=sum(x@I)@Acount(x@I)@A. mean(x@I)@A=\frac{sum(x@I)@A}{count(x@I)@A}.

The displayed mean does not retain the weights represented by COUNT. It can remain an established scalar result even when that basis is no longer retained.

A family can be sufficient for another family without being self-sufficient for its own continuation. For instance, under a fixed variance convention,

stddev(x@I;ddof=d)@A=variance(x@I;ddof=d)@A. stddev(x@I;ddof=d)@A=\sqrt{variance(x@I;ddof=d)@A}.

An established variance at the same anchor can construct standard deviation. This does not authorize averaging or otherwise combining finalized variance values to reconstruct variance at another anchor.

Similarly, exact distinct count can be constructed from an admitted set-valued family. Multiset-valued measures can retain multiplicity for distributional laws. These are ordinary families with rich semantic values, not a separate layer of analytical state. [2]

5.7 Semantic value structure is not analytical location

A law may require addition, division, equality, value comparison, set or multiset union, a point reference, or a structured value. ToD states those semantic requirements. A concrete type specification determines which value domains supply them and how they are represented.

Internal value structure is not analytical location.

A set, multiset, tuple, vector, matrix, or sketch can be the value of one measure. Its internal elements do not automatically become dimensions or anchor points. A coordinate reference carried inside a FIRST/LAST witness likewise does not give the witness-valued measure a second current anchor.

5.8 Why sufficiency is not defined by an information quotient

The target relativity is already explicit in ℬ∈𝒮(F)\mathcal B\in\mathcal S(F): the basis is sufficient for the target under its admitted law and domain. It makes no general claim about arbitrary future restrictions, pairings, contextual reformation, or other targets.

This formulation does not adopt the continuation-class-relative information quotient explored in earlier unpublished Measure Algebra drafts as a ToD primitive or as the normative definition of sufficiency. The obligations here are family-law correctness, independently establishable bases, conserved formation and contribution, well-founded dependency, and justified use-specific substitution. Compatible earlier work on constitutive anchors, closed law contracts, participation, pairing, and carrier adequacy remains applicable. [2, 3, 4]

6.1.1 The inherited nonempty semigroup case

Version 7.0 also admits self-sufficient families whose continuation is an associative and commutative semigroup, with no identity element. That standing is retained. The monoid formulation of Proposition 6.1 is a useful proof fragment, not a restriction of all families to monoids. [2, §§5.2, 7.1]

For a nonempty finite contributing fiber DA(a)D_A(a), the same regrouping argument applies to the nonempty intermediate fibers indexed by BD(a)B_D(a). Each inner fold and the outer fold then contain at least one contribution. Associativity and commutativity give the same direct/staged equality without using an identity element. Known-empty intermediate contribution blocks can be excluded from this nonempty fold by the established participation rule; blocks with unknown support cannot be treated as known empty.

If the target contributing fiber is empty, the semigroup law supplies no fold value. Eligibility, support, or a separately declared completion determines the result. MIN and MAX over an ordinary ordered value domain are the standard examples. This recovers the published nonempty case while leaving Proposition 6.1 and the witness-monoid proofs unchanged.

6.2 Composite coherence

Proposition 6.2 — Coherence through a sufficient-state basis. Suppose a target family FF is constructed from an admitted, well-founded basis ℬ\mathcal B. If the basis measures at AA are path-independent under the relevant contracts, and the same governed constructor is defined on them, then the constructed F@AF@A is path-independent along those basis-construction paths.

Proof. Each path establishes the same role-indexed basis input. Applying the same deterministic semantic constructor to those inputs yields the same target. Induction through the well-founded dependency structure extends the argument to nested composite bases. ▫\square

This proves coherence within an admitted construction. Comparing different bases also requires §5.3. It does not prove that arbitrary changes of analytical law commute, nor that every materialization of a composite family retains the required basis.

6.3 Same family does not imply mutual reachability

Family coherence does not erase partition geometry. Revenue at Week and Revenue at Month may belong to one additive family even when neither anchor refines the other. The family guarantees agreement along admitted paths; it does not manufacture a path between incomparable locations.

Likewise, analytical derivability does not imply derivability from every stored representation. A source or alternative basis may establish a target even when one proposed materialized path has lost necessary state. Sections 9–10 separate these availability and reuse questions from the coherence theorem. [2]

6.4 Identity-relative consistency

Let C(d)C(d) be the canonical analytical identity established by a derivation. When

C(d1)=C(d2)=F@A, C(d_1)=C(d_2)=F@A,

the two derivations claim the same analytical object. They must agree under the compatible instance and contracts that license both. If their canonical identities differ, equality of their values is not required. A disagreement between incompatible snapshots is not a counterexample to a theorem whose premises fix one coherent instance.

For exact realizations, agreement is equality in the declared semantic domain. An approximate realization of the same target must instead satisfy its explicitly governed approximation/error contract; literal equality of approximate outputs is not silently imposed, and exact equality is not silently weakened. Sections 10.9 and 12.1 locate and constrain that realization standing.

The order of reasoning matters. Current numerical equality does not merge two identities; identity determines which consistency obligation applies. Section 5.3 separately requires each admitted basis to construct the target correctly on its own domain. [1, 2]

6.5 Compressing an analytical path

Where family coherence establishes that intermediate staging is immaterial, a coherent family-internal path can be represented in canonical ancestry by the family identity rather than by every intermediate step. For additive Revenue, a path through Day can be equivalent to direct continuation to Month under the same contributions and contracts.

An intervening family-changing construction is different. MAX begun at Day cannot simply be moved before the formation of daily Revenue. The original Day anchor is constitutive of that family. Canonical compression removes only distinctions that governed equivalence makes immaterial; it does not erase meaning-bearing formation, order, restriction, or participation.

The family provides local coherence. Lineage preserves the constitutive ancestry that coherence does not remove. [1, 2]

6.6 Equivalent state representations and sufficient-state alternatives

Version 6.1 required a declared family-state equivalence to respect combination and the family finalizer. The useful obligation survives the Version 7 account of state as ordinary family measures; it does not require the rejected information-quotient construction. [1, §6.9]

For a self-sufficient state family GG, suppose two representations are declared semantically equivalent as continuation inputs, written u≡Gu′u\equiv_G u'. On the same compatible instance and admitted domain, the equivalence must be a congruence:

u≡Gu′,v≡Gv′⇒u⊕Gv≡Gu′⊕Gv′. u\equiv_G u',\quad v\equiv_G v' \quad\Longrightarrow\quad u\oplus_G v\equiv_G u'\oplus_G v'.

u≡Gu′⇒ϕ(u)=ϕ(u′) u\equiv_G u'\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \phi(u)=\phi(u')

where the same governed constructor ϕ\phi is defined on both inputs. Literal semantic equality is sufficient; alternative encodings need an established equality interpretation or admitted conversion.

This is not a requirement that every sufficient-state basis for a target be interchangeable. RR, WW, and scalar LL may retain different information. Proposition 8.2 justifies a particular one-way compression; §§5.3 and 10.5 govern the target correctness and later uses of other substitutions. Equal displayed results alone do not establish continuation-state equivalence.

7. Governed order on analytical points

7.1 Two levels of order

For the multidimensional construction admitted in Version 7.1, governance identifies the constituent dimensions A1,…,AnA_1,\ldots,A_n of the constitutive anchor SS. It establishes a complete point order <i<_i within each AiA_i and a precedence permutation

This is the specified order construction for the present revision. It is not a mathematical assertion that every possible order on a product must be lexicographic. No additional order constructors are admitted here.

7.2 What belongs to identity

Write 𝒪S\mathcal O_S for the selected governed constituent orders and their precedence at SS. An ordered family fixes 𝒪S\mathcal O_S as constitutive law. Changing the order in a meaning-bearing way changes the family construction. Reordering display columns or execution fragments does not.

An equivalent order representation can replace another when the relevant equivalence is established over the declared scope. Agreement of current winners is insufficient. Different order definitions over the same anchor may coexist; the family must resolve which one it uses.

A one-dimensional anchor makes precedence trivial. A global multidimensional ordered support requires both levels of the construction. Day chronology within separately governed fixed-Customer contexts does not silently establish Customer priority across those contexts.

7.3 No generic tie rule in the foundational construction

Two distinct analytical points of SS are distinguished by its complete governed order. Several carrier records contributing to one SS-point are not several tied analytical points. They first require a lawful, coherent account of the operand at that point.

If a physical priority value or truncated label maps distinct points to the same comparison representation, that representation has not realized the declared complete point order. Appending a storage identifier or relying on sort stability is not a repair of analytical law.

The complete order also does not prove that every requested infinite fiber has an extremum. The FIRST/LAST proof below is for finite contribution fibers. It preserves the complete-order requirement and states the finite scope explicitly.

For example, with s1<s2<s3<s4s_1<s_2<s_3<s_4, the intermediate groups {s1,s4}\{s_1,s_4\} and {s2,s3}\{s_2,s_3\} retain s4s_4 and s3s_3. Their combined winner is s4s_4. The groups need not be consecutive.

If a different ordered family is newly constituted at BB, it needs its own governed order at that constitutive anchor. This is a different formation question, not a prerequisite for continuing WW.

8.5 The witness need not have one physical format

An exact coordinate tuple, a governed point reference, or a certified order-preserving encoding may realize the required witness. A carrier is sufficient only if it preserves enough identity and comparison information for the admitted law.

An optimization that can reconstruct the winner from other retained governed facts can be valid. It must establish recoverability rather than infer it from the displayed value. “Generally insufficient” does not mean “never recoverable under any stronger contract.”

9. Evidence adequacy is relative to the law

9.1 Definition, evidence, and realization

Three judgments must remain distinct:

Judgment Question
Analytical definition and derivation Is the requested analytical object determinate and is the proposed derivation admitted?
Evidence adequacy Do current governed facts establish this instance of that object?
Realization fidelity Does the physical computation preserve the resolved analytical request and its required guarantee?

These judgments are not new services or ontological primitives. They distinguish premises already used by the theory. A complete order definition can coexist with missing comparison evidence. A lawful sum can coexist with missing required inputs. A successful sort can coexist with an ungoverned order. [8]

9.2 A result need not require every possible input value

Suppose the complete eligible domain in a target fiber is established as {d1,d2,d3}\{d_1,d_2,d_3\}, with d1<d2<d3d_1<d_2<d_3. Suppose the value at d3d_3 is supported. The earlier operand values are unavailable.

LAST can still be established. Every assignment to the earlier values leaves the selected point and its supported value unchanged. This does not establish SUM, which generally depends on the unavailable values.

More generally, sufficient evidence for the exact LAST witness consists of an established candidate s*s^*, its membership and eligibility in the intended target fiber, an established maximality claim over that fiber, and its supported operand value, under the fixed law and compatible context. The witness is then (s*,f(s*))(s^*,f(s^*)), and its value constructs scalar LAST. Every participating point is no later than s*s^*, so unavailable nonwinning operand values cannot change that conclusion.

The maximality claim may follow from a complete domain account or another governed proof excluding any later eligible contribution. Its premises must cover the intended participation rule, not merely the surviving observations. No physical guess substitutes for that proof.

This is a dependency-specific justification. It does not introduce an information-quotient ontology or a general automatic sufficiency procedure.

9.3 What cannot be silently changed

If the selected eligible point’s value is unsupported, ordinary LAST over eligible points cannot retreat to the previous supported value. LAST over supported observations has a different participation rule.

If the existence of a later eligible point is unresolved, a latest observed value does not by itself establish LAST. If placement under the required ordered anchor is unresolved, assigning the record a backend null position does not establish its participation.

A known lost record can establish event existence while leaving some values or coordinates unsupported. The failed dependencies are determined from the surviving evidence, not from the phrase “lost record” alone. A valid coarse total can survive lost Day placement; a daily sequence requiring that placement may not.

These distinctions do not require a universal linear sequence of checks. The needed dependencies come from the requested law. Unsupported eligibility is not automatically ineligibility, and lack of support is not necessarily an established negative observation about existence.

9.4 Downstream disclosure cannot repair an undefined computation

An ordered scan can use an unplaceable value before the final frame withholds its row. Removing that row later does not remove its contribution to surviving cumulative or relative-position results.

A disclosure can state a limitation on an otherwise established, correctly described result. It cannot make arbitrary point selection determinate. Silently restricting the domain and then disclosing “incomplete data” does not, by itself, establish that the returned numbers denote the originally requested family.

A separately governed restricted result may be available. Its target and conditions must be explicit; this theory revision does not invent a serving policy or authorize a specific wire outcome.

9.5 Coarser support need not require every finer witness to be available

Let the intended participating points be established as s1<s2<s3s_1<s_2<s_3, with values at s2s_2 and s3s_3 supported as 2020 and 3030, while the value at s1s_1 is unavailable. Let an intermediate anchor have contributing blocks {s1}\{s_1\} and {s2,s3}\{s_2,s_3\}.

The first intermediate witness is unavailable: its selected value is not established. Nevertheless the combined target witness is (s3,30)(s_3,30), established directly from the complete participating order and the supported maximal value. A complete retained point-value set at that target is unavailable as well; the compressed witness is still established.

This does not authorize treating the unavailable intermediate witness as ⊥\bot. A plan that specifically consumes all exact intermediate witnesses lacks one of its inputs. A different admitted derivation can establish the coarser witness using the maximality evidence just described. Coherence compares established derivations under their premises; it does not require every possible staged realization to be available whenever the target is supported.

9.6 A sufficient witness is not a necessity theorem for a value-only target

A different admitted proof can sometimes establish a scalar result without establishing the witness family itself. Consider two ordered points p<qp<q. The operand is established as 77 at each. The participation rule definitely includes pp; whether it includes qq is unresolved, and these are the exhaustive possible participants in the target fiber.

Under that already specified LAST law the nonempty scalar result is 77 either way. The winner’s point identity is not established. Reporting L@A=7L@A=7 under this complete argument does not license reporting either (p,7)(p,7) or (q,7)(q,7) as W@AW@A.

This is a counterexample to claiming that the witness basis is always necessary, not a new generic evaluation policy. The argument requires known nonemptiness, exhaustive coverage of possible participants, and supported equal operand values at every possible winner. Equal currently observed values without those premises are insufficient. The example does not create family identity from numerical agreement; the target law and identity were fixed before the evidence argument.

If that scalar is stored, the materialization can record the established scalar target and the justification just given. It cannot record an established witness or inherit witness-continuation rights merely because that witness would be sufficient for the scalar. The same scalar family identity applies whether the value was established through a witness or through another admitted argument; the justification and retained information differ. Section 10.7 makes this distinction explicit.

10. Materialization, reuse, and change

10.1 Reuse is a separate analytical claim

A stored G@BG@B is useful for a target only where the target law admits it as sufficient state and the retained instance satisfies the reuse conditions. The same family label and value type are not enough.

The relevant obligations include family/order compatibility, compatible evidence and evaluation context, matching participation and formation, correct contribution accounting, admitted movement, and enough retained state for the requested operation. These are obligations, not a mandatory physical schema.

The four governance locations in §1.5 determine which facts belong to family identity, movement validity, certificates/materializations, or metadata. A display label does not enter law merely because it is stored beside state. A snapshot need not become a new family identity, but incompatible snapshots cannot silently be merged as one coherent instance.

10.2 Regrouping is not arbitrary restriction

Suppose s1<s2<s3s_1<s_2<s_3. Two input domains, {s1,s3}\{s_1,s_3\} and {s2,s3}\{s_2,s_3\}, can have the same retained LAST witness at s3s_3. Excluding s3s_3 produces different winners. The original witness cannot determine both restricted answers.

Therefore sufficiency for coarsening does not imply sufficiency for a later value filter, population restriction, or redefinition of formation. A source reread, richer retained state, or a separate recovery theorem may be needed.

10.3 Mergeability is not deletion-maintainability

Combining additional compatible contributions is not the same operation as deleting or correcting a previous winner. A LAST or MAX witness can forget which point would win after its selected point is removed.

The same caution applies to nonwinning values if a later operation changes the order definition. State sufficient under one order need not be sufficient to reselect under another. A law-level version change cannot be implemented by merely relabeling old winners.

10.4 Overlap and idempotence

For additive state, combining summaries over {i1,i2}\{i_1,i_2\} and {i2,i3}\{i_2,i_3\} double-counts i2i_2 unless an admitted correction accounts for that overlap. Algebraic associativity never authorizes unknown multiplicity.

Witness extrema and set union are idempotent on coherent identical contributions. This can support stronger reuse in a particular admitted law. It does not give every family permission to deduplicate, mix inconsistent copies, or disregard contribution provenance.

The general finite theorem uses the same correctly accounted contributions on direct and staged paths. A physical combine with other accounting must establish its own equivalence.

10.5 Result agreement is not continuation-equivalent state

A conversion between bases must be justified for the use being claimed. Agreement of displayed target values at BB alone does not prove that converted state can replace the original in a later construction at AA.

For example, MEAN states (10,1)(10,1) and (1000,100)(1000,100) both display 1010. Adding the same new state (20,1)(20,1) gives means 1515 and 1020/1011020/101. The equal current results did not retain equal continuation information. These are two possible compatible inputs considered separately, not permission to mix incompatible snapshots.

Proposition 8.2 provides the stronger fact needed for the specific R→WR\to W conversion: extracting the witness before compatible union agrees with extracting it afterward. That is why this compression preserves FIRST/LAST continuation. A scalar rendering establishes only its scalar target unless another recoverability or continuation result applies.

The requirement is operation-specific fidelity, not a demand that all bases be mutually reversible or share one carrier type. No general information-quotient construction is required.

10.6 Lossy state does not certify the consistency premise

Let p<qp<q. One retained input claims (p,10)(p,10) and (q,20)(q,20); another claims (p,11)(p,11) in what is asserted to be the same coherent context. The first input compresses to (q,20)(q,20), which no longer exposes its (p,10)(p,10) claim. Combining that winner with (p,11)(p,11) can return (q,20)(q,20) without discovering the contradiction.

A local equal-point check is not enough to solve this. Comparing the two pp-witnesses first finds a conflict; comparing one of them with qq first can discard it and conceal the conflict. An error-reporting rule added to lossy merge is therefore not an associative global validator over arbitrary conflicting evidence.

There is no contradiction in Proposition 8.1: these inputs violate its coherent-instance premise. There is a practical obligation to bind reused state to adequate context and compatibility evidence rather than claim that the compressed witness proves that premise by itself. This does not require every witness to retain all source records, nor does it specify a certificate format. It states what must be established outside the lossy value combination before its theorem can be invoked.

10.7 A stored answer and retained sufficient state are different claims

A materialization must distinguish what analytical content it retains from the target results that content can establish. It must also retain or reference the justification and governing context for those claims. A catalog entry saying only “LAST at AA is available” is insufficient when it is used to choose a continuation plan: it does not say whether the available object is the scalar target L@AL@A, the witness-valued measure W@AW@A, or other admitted evidence for the target.

The distinction is directional. On the declared nonempty constructor domain, an adequately established witness supports the governed projection

W@A→valueL@A. W@A\xrightarrow{\operatorname{value}}L@A.

The converse does not hold in general. A scalar can be established without establishing the witness (§9.6), and storing a scalar produced from a witness does not imply that the witness remains retained or recoverable (§8.5). Thus the two objects are not freely interchangeable, even though the witness can lawfully supply the scalar target. This does not make their information incomparable or prohibit the value projection.

Three materialization situations illustrate the consequence:

The last two rows can have the same family identity and scalar value. They are not new families classified by proof method. Their establishment records differ. Likewise, one physical artifact can hold both W@AW@A and L@AL@A, or hold W@AW@A while making L@AL@A derivable without storing it separately. These are representational choices, provided the analytical claims remain distinguishable.

For any proposed use, the materialization record and governing definitions must jointly establish the following information: the identities and semantic content actually retained; the precise claim supported by that content; its justification and applicable evidence context; and the admitted derivation or continuation supported by the retained information. The justification may be referenced rather than copied into every record. Reuse permissions may be derived from governed law rather than enumerated as catalog fields. No particular physical schema is required.

A known-empty witness remains distinct from a scalar LAST result: the initial scalar law has no eligible value on that empty fiber (§8.3). Nor does a historical derivation record automatically make its old inputs currently available. If retained references or additional evidence permit exact witness recovery, that recovery can be admitted on its own premises; it is not inferred from the scalar’s presence or from the fact that a witness was once computed.

A planner may use a supported witness to answer a scalar request through the admitted value projection. It may also use a scalar established by another admitted argument to answer that same scalar request. A plan that specifically combines witness state, however, needs that state or a separately justified replacement or recovery. A claim that the scalar answer is available does not satisfy the witness input requirement. Conversely, failure to obtain one witness basis does not refute an independently established scalar answer.

The boundary in §10.6 still applies: naming an evidence context or attaching a derivation reference is not, by itself, proof that two retained objects are compatible. The evidence supporting the particular reuse must establish the required premises. Equally, a justification for the current scalar target need not retain enough information to validate or perform every future operation.

A materialization must state what it retains, what claim is established and why, and what reuse is justified. Availability of a scalar answer does not imply retention of its sufficient state; availability of sufficient state can justify an answer through its admitted constructor.

This requirement uses the existing distinctions among family identity, sufficient-state relations, evidence, and materialization. It introduces no additional ontological kind.

10.8 Physical storage and analytical materializability

Any measure can in principle be physically stored. Analytical materializability is a narrower reuse claim. The inherited v7 distinction is:

Self-materializable: a family is self-sufficient under its admitted continuation law, so retained measures at appropriate finer anchors can participate in constructing that family at a coarser anchor.

Materializable for a target: a family occurs in an admitted sufficient-state basis of another target. Its established measure at the specified anchor can participate in the target constructor on the basis’s admitted domain. That does not confer its own cross-anchor continuation.

A family with either standing is analytically materializable in this sense. These are roles of ordinary families, not new kinds of stored objects. Revenue, COUNT, distinct sets, multisets, and the extremum witness illustrate self-sufficient continuation. A finalized variance illustrates same-anchor sufficiency for standard deviation without self-sufficient variance continuation. [2]

This terminology does not deny that a scalar answer can be stored and served for its own justified claim. Section 10.7 distinguishes that availability from the additional reuse promised by an analytical materialization. Nor does analytical materializability claim that a particular representation is economical.

10.9 Approximation remains explicit

An approximate realization may use a sketch-valued self-sufficient family with a governed commutative merge law. The sketch family is an ordinary analytical family whose measures carry sketch values; it is not exact distinct_set state.

Where the semantic target remains exact count_distinct, an HLL estimate is an approximate realization of that target. It must carry an explicit certificate/materialization contract describing the approximation and its error standing. It is not an exact sufficient-state basis for count_distinct, because it does not determine the exact target measure. [2, §9.5]

Approximation changes family identity only when the governed declaration changes the semantic target or family law itself. Otherwise its guarantee, error bounds, and retained capability belong to the certificate/materialization location for the same target identity (§1.5). A different realization guarantee alone must not be described as a different analytical quantity.

The converse matters equally: retaining the same target identity does not make an approximate output exact or permit an unqualified substitution for exact state. Exact sufficient-state adequacy remains exact. A physical optimization must preserve the declared law or carry the explicitly admitted approximation/error standing described in §12.1. [1, 2]

10.10 Lawful materialization can improve realization

The exact-distinct example from Version 7.0 illustrates the positive consequence of the reuse discipline. Scalar distinct counts at finer anchors generally cannot support exact rollup because they discard overlap information. Materialized distinct_set(sku@order) measures can be unioned and counted under the same governed identity and participation law.

Where the governed SKU domain is small, a realization may use a dictionary-backed bitmap or another exact set representation. The representation is an implementation choice, not an additional analytical law. It can avoid returning to finer records when the retained state is adequate; no performance guarantee follows from materializability alone.

The dependency is analytical law, sufficient-state family, semantic type capability, and then faithful materialization. This keeps optimization downstream of meaning while explaining why explicit sufficient state is useful. [2, §9.4]

12. Realization and neighboring responsibilities

12.1 Numerical realization and semantic equality

The coherence equations are equations in the declared semantic domains. Their interpretation must not silently change when a backend uses finite-precision arithmetic.

For example, evaluating binary floating-point operations in the two groupings

(1016+(−1016))+1and1016+((−1016)+1) (10^{16}+(-10^{16}))+1 \quad\text{and}\quad 10^{16}+((-10^{16})+1)

can yield 11 and 00. Finite-precision decimal arithmetic can also break associativity when precision is insufficient. [11]

An exact realization must preserve the required semantic operation on its admitted domain. Schematically, with a decoding function from valid physical states,

decode(û⊕̂v̂)=decode(û)⊕decode(v̂). \operatorname{decode}(\widehat u\ \widehat\oplus\ \widehat v) = \operatorname{decode}(\widehat u)\oplus\operatorname{decode}(\widehat v).

An approximation contract must instead state the relation or bound it preserves and how that guarantee composes. Deterministic computation alone is not exactness. A numerical tolerance is not, by itself, an equivalence relation for analytical identity or a proof that errors remain within bounds after arbitrary staging. Version 7.0 already states this realization obligation: a realization that neither preserves the required law nor declares the applicable approximation/error regime is nonconforming, however small its observed error. Numerical stabilization alone does not turn an inexact operation into an exact associative one. [2, §7.1]

ToD states this obligation. CDT and realization specifications provide the concrete numerical laws, representations, and guarantees. This manuscript does not invent a CDT capability name, claim an existing implementation conforms, or replace the statistical meaning of an approximation with an execution flag.

12.2 Semantic types and analytical order

ToD states the semantic capabilities a law requires. CDT or another type specification defines concrete domains, equality, comparison, arithmetic, structured values, sets, multisets, and extension representations. An ordered point representation must realize the governed point order; an incidental database collation is not analytical authority.

A witness can be represented by a suitable coordinate tuple, governed point reference, or adequate order-preserving encoding. The theory does not require a nominal built-in type called Witness. It requires a representation and comparison contract faithful to the law. A comparison capability alone does not determine which business priority or dimension precedence constitutes a family.

Carrier nullability and conversion errors likewise do not define analytical missingness. The applicable realization must retain enough information to distinguish the law’s empty, unsupported, ineligible, and undefined cases rather than substitute a physical convention for them.

12.3 Manifold declarations and assurance

A Manifold supplies a governed instance of analytical definitions: universes, anchor structures, family laws, particular orders, and their relevant authority and evidence. It does not establish mathematical coherence by naming a declaration or establish current empirical premises merely by having once ratified a definition.

Structural conformance is not institutional authority. A conforming selected publication must make its analytical objects, constitutive laws, sufficient-state bases, lineage, edge contracts, and relevant certificates explicit and internally resolvable, and satisfy the theory’s well-formedness requirements. The surrounding governance process determines which conforming publication has authority for an organization or use. A human’s selection of a publication does not replace its conformance obligations. [1, Appendix A; 2, §1.3]

Version 7.0 places declaration assurance upstream of analytical derivation. Once a Manifold is assured, query execution must not silently become a second, competing declaration validator. The planner should adjudicate the requested derivation against those assured laws and check current conditions within their declared scope, not reconstruct or reinterpret missing law from physical bindings. A stale or contradicted assurance premise cannot authorize a result merely because the declaration was once accepted. [2, §10.5]

Declaration assurance, current evidence adequacy, and runtime admission therefore remain distinguishable. A realization failure does not retroactively redefine a lawful target. The theory states which premises assurance must support without prescribing its certificate schema, institutional roles, or service lifecycle. [8]

12.4 Request languages and general expressions

Frame-QL is one request language built against this analytical foundation. It can name families, request anchored measures, combine expressions, and articulate contextual operations and output selection. The theory does not require that language, nor does it release its grammar.

The general expression layer is broader than family formation. Arithmetic, predicates, LAG, rank, rolling and cumulative calculations, and frame operations need their own semantics. A value-returning expression is not automatically a family. A contextual formation is not automatically excluded from a subsequently governed family either (§3.4).

FIRST/LAST notation can denote the admitted ordered family construction when its constitutive anchor, order, participation, and law are established. Other uses of the same word need not denote that family. Function-name classification and parser acceptance do not decide admission.

Query-level output ordering is distinct from the order constituting an analytical family. Sorting and limiting an already-formed frame can select displayed points without forming the same analytical object as an inner LAST expression. Numerical agreement does not erase that distinction. Request-local restriction also differs from declaring a new universe or population; the governing restriction or passage contract decides what changed.

A resolved explanation and a physical execution must refer to the same analytical meaning. An explanation that describes an ideal order while execution follows unrelated legacy rules does not establish fidelity. A language may implement a sound fragment of the theory without implementing all of it. The predecessor cites Frame-QL: An Introduction, Version 2.3, as its companion language account; mathematical forms in this manuscript do not assert new shipped syntax. [2, 8, 14]

12.5 Statistical and decision claims

A deterministic statistic can be a governed fact about the data target established by its analytical law. A population or sample denominator convention is itself an analytical distinction. Choosing ddof=1ddof=1, for example, does not by itself establish an inference beyond that target.

A claim about a larger population, a future period, a latent process, another regime, or a causal effect needs additional evidence and assumptions. Those crossings belong to the Statistical Bridge and neighboring frameworks, not to a more permissive reading of family coherence. Likewise, a mathematically established measure does not by itself authorize a business action. [7, 8]

14. Conclusion

Analytical data is more than a value carried by a storage structure. A universe establishes which root points exist. An anchor gives a governed partition of those points. A datum binds a typed value to one analytical point. A measure family gives analytical identity; a measure is that family at one current anchor:

F@A. \boxed{F@A.}

Formation records what the family’s analytical input means. The family contract specifies the admitted anchors, movements, participation, value capabilities, sufficient-state constructions, and exceptional cases. Lineage preserves constitutive ancestry. Coherence determines when alternative admitted derivations must agree.

Sufficient state belongs to the same analytical world as the results it supports. SUM and COUNT can construct MEAN. A set-valued family can construct exact distinct count. A winning-point/value family can construct scalar FIRST or LAST. These bases have distinct laws, domains, and retained information. Each must correctly construct its target; no basis becomes necessary merely because it is sufficient.

A governed analytical order may be constitutive of a family. It is not the incidental enumeration in which evidence arrives. Complete constituent point orders and their precedence order the existing multidimensional point space without fabricating absent combinations. FIRST/LAST witness continuation then satisfies the ordinary finite commutative-state theorem. It is a specialization of the family model, not a competing ontology.

Evidence adequacy and reuse remain separate from that mathematical result. A target can be established while a particular basis or intermediate materialization is unavailable. A stored scalar is not automatically retained continuation state. A lossy witness is not proof of the coherence of inputs it has discarded. The mathematical law, its empirical premises, and its physical realization must each support the claim being made.

The practical aim remains focused:

Know which analytical points exist, where a measure lives, which family gives it identity, how its input was formed, what state its admitted continuations require, and which evidence and realization conditions justify the result and its reuse.

That foundation does not replace business knowledge, semantic types, query languages, databases, statistical inference, or judgment. It gives those systems an explicit analytical object to describe, establish, and preserve.

B.2 Datum, family, and measure

A datum is one governed typed value at an analytical point. A family FF is a governed analytical identity with a specified target, determinate formation, admitted anchors and movements, and sufficient-state constructions coherent under their contracts. B.7 Evidence adequacy

At an established anchor, SF,A⊆EF,A⊆AS_{F,A}\subseteq E_{F,A}\subseteq A, with supported map mF,A:SF,A→VFm_{F,A}:S_{F,A}\to V_F. Existence, required placement, and eligibility may themselves lack supporting evidence. The law determines which dependencies are required for the target.

Lossy witness compression cannot by itself establish that all discarded inputs were coherent. The coherent-instance premise requires adequate independent context and compatibility evidence.

B.8 Materialization and realization

A family is self-materializable when its own admitted continuation is self-sufficient. It is materializable for a target when it supplies a role in an admitted basis of that target. Physical storage of an answer does not confer either additional permission merely by existing.

A materialization and the governing definitions must jointly establish what content is retained, what target is justified and why, and what reuse is licensed. On the nonempty domain, W@A→L@AW@A\to L@A by value projection is valid. The reverse is not generally available. A stored scalar, a historical witness derivation, and currently retained or recoverable witness state are different claims.

Reuse must preserve admitted formation, participation, contribution accounting, context, order, and state. Sufficiency for coarsening does not imply sufficiency for arbitrary restrictions or deletions. Physical realization must preserve the declared semantic equality or approximation guarantee. Approximation for an unchanged target is certificate/materialization standing; family identity changes only when the semantic target or identity-bearing family law changes. A sketch estimate does not become exact sufficient state by retaining the same target name.

B.9 Identity-relative consistency and scope

If two established derivations claim the same F@AF@A under compatible premises, consistency is required. Different canonical identities need not have equal values; equal observed values do not create identity. Family membership does not imply mutual reachability between incomparable anchors.

These are conditional analytical claims. They do not prove empirical completeness, arbitrary family admission, every statistical convention, backend numerical conformance, or an inferential claim beyond the governed data target.
