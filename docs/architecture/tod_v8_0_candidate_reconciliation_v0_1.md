## 1. The candidate architecture, tested piece by piece

### 1.1 Manifold constitutes; it does not originate universal law

**Survives, and it is already the corpus's position.** MA v1.0 §7: the preserve/establish distinction is
*"determined **ex ante** by governed family identity; agreement among computed outputs cannot create identity
after the fact."* ToD §4's contract is a list of **contingent** responsibilities. Nothing in the corpus has a
family originating a law.

**One sharpening the candidate should carry:** the Manifold's contingency is exactly *"could have been
otherwise."* A universal law could not have been otherwise, which is why citing one is contingent while the law
is not. **That is the line, and it is what makes `source`'s two kinds exhaustive.**

### 1.2 Universe establishes analytical existence and geometry

**Survives**, and the 2026-09-15 ruling has already consolidated it more tightly than the candidate states:

> **Constituted — three governed facts and nothing else:** the identity of the analytical world; the **primitive
> root-point individuation** (a *closed* set of governed constituents, each with a governed reference and value
> domain, **order not identity-bearing**); and **\(\lambda_U\)**, determinate and internally resolvable.
>
> **Derived — never authored, never declared, never fingerprinted:** \(\Omega_U\) · the root anchor \(R_U\) · the
> scalar anchor · every Case-S coordinate placement and its fiber partition · every compound anchor · the
> refinement order among all of them.
>
> **Named — a third standing:** a governed conventional name *resolving to* a derived partition; established,
> never inferred; non-constitutive.

> ⚡ **So the candidate's geometry list is right but mis-levelled.** Root-point existence is **constituted**
> (via \(\lambda_U\)); anchors, refinement, projection, projection fibers, common refinement and incomparability
> are **all derived** from the individuation. **The candidate should say: the universe constitutes three things
> and the geometry follows.** That is one assumption where the candidate implies several.

**Case-G placements are outside the constitution** — *"a structure **on** \(\Omega_U\) that does not change
\(\Omega_U\), and therefore requires its own governed act."* So a placement is **governed universe-level
structure that is not part of the individuation**, which is a fourth standing the candidate's list omits.

### 1.3 One family, one universe

**Candidate invariant: every measure family belongs to exactly one universe.**

Tested in §2 below against ToD's own statements. **It survives**, it is **not** explicitly published, and it
**earns its keep** in three independent places:

- it is what makes `F @ A` resolvable at all — *anchor identity is universe-relative*, so `A` is meaningless
  without a universe, and the only place to get one is \(F\);
- it is what lets a **material measure drop \(U\)** (§8);
- it is what makes *"one MEL expression is universe-local"* a **consequence rather than a second rule** (§4).

> **Recommendation: v8 should state it explicitly as an axiom, because the corpus relies on it and never says
> it.** That is an assumption *added*, and it pays for itself three times.

### 1.4 Measure = `F @ A`

**Retained unchanged.** No second governed measure object is introduced. §7 withdraws the one I had been
drifting toward.

### 1.5 Analytical law: universal, applied universe-locally, cited constitutively

**Survives, and this is the candidate's strongest single move.** Three published anchors:

- **MA v1.0 §4.1** — the state-law taxonomy is a property of *the declared state law*, not of an operator name:
  *"**The declared state law determines the admissible composition. Current implementation behavior does not
  define the state law.**"*
- **MA v1.0 §6** — *"Until such a formation and participation law is declared, `corr` is **not** an operator of
  the proved Measure Algebra. It is a candidate extension whose **numerical implementation would not be enough to
  admit it**."* A law exists independently of any family and independently of any engine.
- **CC §5.1** — the fiber is *"the preimage of the grouping map, not the row container used to implement it."*

**The three-way split is exactly right, and it explains something v7.1 leaves implicit:** why a family
declaration is small. It is small because **the family contributes only the citation and its parameters**; the
law is universal and the geometry is the universe's.

> ⚠ **One qualification the candidate must carry.** *Applications are universe-local* is true of the **operands**
> — they resolve in one universe — but the **premises of a law can be facts about the universe's geometry**
> (order-compatibility, rectangularity, projection existence). So the correct statement is: **a law is universal;
> its premises may be universe-local facts; its application is to measures of one universe; and the citation is
> the family's contingent act.** Four parts, not three.

### 1.6 Family declaration and 1.7 Participation

**Both survive**, verified in the two preceding units and unchanged here. `clause = source × scope`; participation
is authored in exactly two places, both inside `source` — a world source's eligibility, and a multi-operand law
source's co-participation rule. **A composition clause authors neither.**

### 1.8 Geometry versus contribution

**Survives, with the published caveat already recorded:** the corpus contains **two non-identical
formalisations** of the same intent —

| | index set | how \(E\), \(S\) enter |
|---|---|---|
| **CC \(G_1\) §15.4** | \(\operatorname{Fib}_q(a')\cap P\) — **index-total** | a three-way **case-split embedding** plus counts \((e,o)\) |
| **ToD §6.1** | \(D\cap\pi^{-1}(a)\) — **index-partial** | \(D\) is a governed participating domain |

Both published; they agree on values wherever \(D=E\); they differ in what is carried through the fold and where
the empty case is decided. **v8 must choose one as normative and derive the other, and it should say which.**
**Recommendation: ToD §6.1's index-partial form is the normative one** — it is the one that makes *participation*
a first-class semantic object, and CC's embedding is then its realization-facing presentation, which is the right
direction of derivation given that CC's counts exist to serve \(\operatorname{Covered}_h\).

### 1.9 Support

**Survives verbatim**, with the one correction recorded previously: support is **carried by** the reduction (CC
\(C_1\), and the \((e,o)\) counts folded alongside the value state), not downstream of it. **What is downstream
is the decision.** The prohibition list — absence of evidence may not redefine existence, eligibility,
participation, contribution, zero, or empty fiber — is exactly the corpus's own, in ToD §2.3, §6.1, §9.3, CC
§15.5 and Frame-QL §8.9.

## 2. The missingness / state taxonomy

**The eight-state account is tested against ToD v7.1, Contract Calculus \(G_0\)/\(G_1\), Measure Algebra and the
Statistical Bridge. It survives — and the gap is sharper than previously characterized.**

| # | state | ToD v7.1 | CC \(G_1\) embedding (§15.4) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **nonexistent** \(\omega\notin\Omega_U\) | §2.1.1's \(\lambda_U\) | \(a\notin P\) — no case; outside the fold |
| 2 | **unresolved existence** | §2.3 — *"Evidence about existence can itself be insufficient… An existing root point's placement under a particular anchor can also be unsupported"* | **no slot** |
| 3 | **ineligible** | §2.3 — *"present but ineligible for a quantity"* | \((0_\kappa,0,0)\) |
| 4 | **unresolved eligibility** | §2.3 — *"**Eligibility may be unresolved rather than established or denied.** These are dependencies to preserve, not instructions to create one universal standing enum"* | **no slot** |
| 5 | **non-participating** | §4.2's \(D\) — *"participation is selected by the law and the resolved request"* | **conflated with 7** |
| 6 | **participating with zero** | §2.3 — *"supported with value zero"* | \((\eta(v),1,1)\) |
| 7 | **participating but unsupported** | §2.3 — *"eligible but unsupported"*; §6.1 — *"must not be replaced by the identity"* | \((0_\kappa,1,0)\) |
| 8 | **known-empty contributing fiber** | §6.1 — the identity applies *"to a **known** empty contribution fiber"* | \(e_q(a')=0\) ⟹ output not eligible |

### 2.1 The gap, characterized precisely rather than repaired

> **Contract Calculus \(G_1\) has four source-point states; the account above needs seven. The shortfall is not
> one omission but two different kinds of omission.**

**(a) Two states have no slot at all** — 2 and 4. CC's embedding is **total on \(P\)** and must know which of its
three cases holds; **an unresolved point cannot be embedded.** And these are genuinely distinct from state 7: in 7
we know the point participates and lack its value; in 2 and 4 **\(D\) itself is unknown**, which is not the same
as \(D\) being empty.

**(b) One CC case silently carries two states** — and this is the sharper finding. **CC has no participating
domain distinct from eligibility.** \(G_1\)'s contract is \((X,U,A,E,S,\beta,\gamma)\): there is an \(E\) and
there is no \(D\). So CC's \(a\in E\setminus S\) covers **both**

- *the law did not consume this point* (state 5) — which should contribute nothing **and not be counted**; and
- *the law consumed it and the value is unestablished* (state 7) — which should be **counted in \(e\) and not in
  \(o\)**.

**They get the same embedding \((0_\kappa,1,0)\).** The consequence is concrete and checkable: under `Complete`
coverage, **a merely non-participating eligible point is demanded as an observation**, so a lawful restricted
construction reports itself under-covered. **That is the same defect as measuring coverage against
\(|Fib\cap E|\) instead of \(|D|\)** — recorded earlier as a recommendation against MA §2.4, and it now has a
second, independent witness.

> **Characterization, not repair:** CC's four states are adequate **exactly where \(D=E\)** — which is the case
> its proved fragments address, and which it never claims to exceed (§12: *"\(G_1\) is the **smallest**
> population-and-partiality extension used here"*; the claims table marks a complete partiality calculus **"Not
> claimed"**). **ToD's \(D\) is the concept CC lacks, and ToD's §2.3 is the standing account CC lacks.** The two
> papers are each complete on their own terms and **have not been composed**.

### 2.2 What the Bridge adds, and it is the motivating case

The Statistical Bridge states the practical form of the same taxonomy, in §1.2: *"Suppose a customer had no
transaction. Did the customer-quarter point exist with Revenue \(=0\)? Did it exist but Revenue was unobserved?
Was the customer ineligible? Did the transaction pipeline miss qualifying records? Did a join remove the row?"* —
and then **§5.1's layering**, which is a distinction none of ToD, CC or MA states:

> \[\text{occurrence or selection}\neq\text{recording}\neq\text{value observation}.\]
> *"A null value or absent row cannot decide which layer failed."*

**That is a three-way split *inside* what ToD calls support**, and §13.3 gives the boundary statement: *"**The
bridge distinguishes an unobserved value at an established eligible point from absence of the point itself.**"*

> **v8 should adopt the eight-state account and should *not* absorb the Bridge's three evidence layers into it.**
> States 1–8 are about **standing**; occurrence / recording / value-observation are three **kinds of evidence
> failure** underneath state 7. Folding them together would lose the Bridge's distinction, which is the one it
> exists to make.

## 3. Event / spine and universe significance — **the candidate's premise needs correcting**

### 3.1 Neither ToD nor the Bridge treats event and spine as universe types

**ToD v7.1 §2.1.1, the decisive sentence:**

> *"Under an occurrence-based law, a point may exist because an event occurred. Under a declared or generated law,
> an operating-calendar store-day can exist independently of a sales observation. **Event-like and spine-like
> domains are forms of the existence law, not additional top-level ontological kinds.**"*

**And the Statistical Bridge says the same thing about itself.** Abstract: *"Event and spine are recurring
**existence forms** under Theory of Data Version 6."* §3.1: *"But **event and spine are existence forms, not the
whole Theory-of-Data universe ontology**."* The Bridge **never uses the term "universe type"**, never enumerates a
closed set, and names no third form.

> ⚡ **So the candidate's premise — *"Statistical Bridge explicitly treats event and spine as universe types"* —
> is not what the Bridge says.** Both authorities call them **forms of the existence law** and both explicitly
> decline the taxonomy reading. **The published distinction is preserved by *not* typing universes**, which is
> the opposite of what preserving it appeared to require.

### 3.2 Where the typed reading actually comes from

It is a **third paper**: `w-missingness-has-a-universe` §4.3 — *"A universe is \(U=(\kappa,A_U,P_U)\) … \(\kappa\)
is the universe type … an event universe is generated by occurrences; a spine universe by declared
expectation."*

**That is the real tension, and it is worth naming precisely: one published paper carries \(\kappa\) as a
component of \(U\); ToD and the Bridge carry the same distinction as a *form of \(\lambda_U\)*.**

**And the estate has already ruled on it.** The 2026-09-15 universe-constitution ruling, §7 — **ruled**, not draft:

> *"**`events | spine | product | registry` are recorded as existing compatibility vocabulary whose eventual
> standing remains to be determined. They must NOT be promoted into \(\lambda_U\) merely because they exist, and
> they are NOT retired or redesigned here.**"*

and §7a: *"`basis` — **a classification of the law's form**; may accompany, never substitutes."*

> **v8 must therefore not ratify a universe-type taxonomy.** It is forbidden by a live ruling, unsupported by
> ToD, and disclaimed by the Bridge. **What v8 should do is state the positive fact all three agree on: the
> constituted object is \(\lambda_U\), and *event-like* and *spine-like* name recurring forms of it.**

### 3.3 The candidate principle, restated so it is true

Huayin's candidate:

> *Universe type may become irrelevant to a particular universal law after that law's premises are established,
> while remaining essential to establishing those premises and to governed passages between universes.*

**The structure is right; the subject is wrong.** Restated:

> **The *form of the existence law* is irrelevant to a universal analytical law once that law's premises are
> established. It remains essential to establishing them — because it determines what a point's absence means —
> and to governed passage between universes.**

**Not falsified, and each clause has an authority:**

| clause | authority |
|---|---|
| irrelevant once premises are established | CC's reducer takes \((X,U,A,E,S,\beta,\gamma)\) and folds; **nothing downstream of the contract consults how \(\Omega_U\) was generated** |
| essential to establishing them | ToD v6.1 — *"In an occurrence-based universe, no qualifying occurrence can mean that no corresponding root point exists. In a declared or generated universe, an expected point can exist even when no observation is available. **The same physical absence therefore need not have the same analytical meaning across universes.**"* |
| essential to passage | Bridge §4.4 — *"establishing a measure on an independently existing spine is a **cross-universe attribution or construction**. **That crossing must state its own contract.**"* |

### 3.4 The two inequalities, both upheld

**`event universe ≠ evidence layer`** — **upheld, and the Bridge is emphatic.** §10.2: *"Login events occupy a
recorded event universe… Their occurrence is distinct from: recording completeness; value observation; status
eligibility; downstream transformation."* §5.1: *"occurrence or selection ≠ recording ≠ value observation."*
**An event universe is a *constitution*; the evidence layers sit underneath it.**

**`spine universe ≠ Cartesian product`** — **upheld, though not from the Bridge**, which says nothing either way
(the words *Cartesian*, *product*, *grid* do not occur in it). The support is:

- **ToD §2.1.3** — *"\(q:S\hookrightarrow\prod_i A_i\). **Its image need not be the full Cartesian product.** The
  universe law determines which root points exist; refinement organizes them. **Neither an order nor a grouping
  operation creates an unoccupied coordinate combination.**"*
- **`w-missingness-has-a-universe` §10.3** — *"A store-day report may be expected only when a store is active. The
  eligibility set is therefore **not the full Cartesian product** of stores and dates."*
- and the Bridge's own spine predicate, §10.1: \(P_S=\{(a,q): a \text{ registered and eligible during } q\}\) —
  **a predicate-restricted set of pairs, not a product.**

> ⚡ **And this is the same fact as §5's rectangularity condition**, which is why it matters here: *the image need
> not fill the product* is precisely the premise an interchange law needs and cannot assume. **One geometric fact,
> two consequences — sparse spines and non-commuting reducers.** That is an assumption *removed*, not added.

### 3.5 Point-in-time, interval, range, membership — where they actually go

**ToD v7.1 contains none of these words.** `interval`, `duration`, `point-in-time`, `range` — **zero occurrences
each.** So they are **not** universe types and **not** geometric structures. They land in two places:

| | where | authority |
|---|---|---|
| **interval / duration / range** | **the semantic value type** | §5.7 — *"**Internal value structure is not analytical location.** A set, multiset, tuple, vector, matrix, or sketch can be the value of one measure. Its internal elements do not automatically become dimensions or anchor points."* |
| **point-in-time** | **not a kind at all** — it is a family with no time-crossing composition clause | the Balance case: point-in-time-ness *is* the absence of a clause |
| **membership** | **a governed structural construction**, which may *produce* a universe | Appendix D — *"a **membership universe** can instead make each membership a distinct point"* — an instance, not a type |

> **So the answer to *"universe types, geometric structures, or something else?"* is: something else in every
> case, and no new kind is needed for any of them.** This is the single largest reduction in independent
> assumptions the candidate achieves.

## 4. MEL and Frame-QL jurisdiction — **report, do not rewrite: both candidates are already law**

Huayin asked that current `FROM`, `AT`, frame-alignment and selected-Manifold rules be **reported** rather than
rewritten. **They do not require one universe for a query.** They require **one Manifold per query** and **one
universe per expression** — which is exactly the candidate.

### 4.1 *One MEL expression is universe-local* — **already law, at the language layer**

The **§2c expression law**, Frame-QL Manual §1.5 and §2.5:

> *"**The universe is resolved STRUCTURALLY, never pinned at query time.** Every column expression evaluates
> inside **exactly one** universe, and a single expression that spans two populations is a `cross_universe`
> **category error, not a hedged answer**."*
>
> *"An expression that combines measures from more than one population is a category error. It returns on the
> query-**error** channel as `cross_universe`, **not as a disposition**."*

And the deeper statement, §1.5 — **the anchor is a pair**:

> *"Every ascription is therefore implicitly `expr @ (a, U)`: the anchor names the coordinates, the universe
> defines the points."*

**Confirmed, with one gap worth recording: this is stated as a *Frame-QL* law and as a Measure-Algebra `MAP1`
premise — *"the inputs remain distinct certified analytical results at the same universe and anchor"* — and
**never as a MEL law or a ToD law**.** There is no MEL specification in the estate.

> **v8 should state it upstream, as a consequence of one-family-one-universe rather than as a language rule.**
> Under that invariant it is not an extra rule at all: an operand is a measure, a measure belongs to a family, a
> family belongs to one universe — so **a universe-crossing expression cannot be written, for the same reason a
> clause cannot name another family's clauses.** That is one assumption removed.

### 4.2 *A frame may assemble multiple universe-local results* — **already law**

Frame-QL Manual §2.5:

> *"A frame in one universe needs no qualification… When several populations genuinely belong in one view, the
> frame **juxtaposes** them — separate series, each evaluating in its own universe, standing together at a shared
> anchor."*

vNext §2.2, *"The output frame is not a new analytical ontology"*:

> *"Two selected expressions may … **even belong to different governed universes where the language permits
> juxtaposition**. **Co-location in one frame does not collapse those identities.**"*

**And the query-level rules are Manifold-scoped, not universe-scoped:**

| rule | what it actually says |
|---|---|
| `FROM` | selects **a Manifold** — *"`FROM` names a Manifold, not a physical table."* Never a universe; **there is no way to name a universe in a query** |
| `AT` | *"a single declaration for the whole query… all series in a query share the output anchor"* — **one anchor, not one universe** |
| co-anchoring | *"A common anchor is a common **(anchor, universe)**, so co-anchoring is checked as **co-universality**"* — per expression |
| selected Manifold | *one* resolved Manifold identity per statement; **per-series** `(anchor, universe)` in the annotation |

> **So the three-layer split the candidate proposes is the estate's own**, and the middle layer already has a
> name: **juxtaposition**.

### 4.3 The third layer — governed passage — and why it must stay separate

Neither composition nor assembly. The Bridge §4.4 supplies the requirement; the estate supplies the shape:
*"Between universes there are exactly two kinds of passage, and both are **declared**: shared grains … faces …
And if neither passage exists? The ask **refuses, and says why**."*

**Three layers, three failure modes, and they are already distinguishable:**

| layer | scope | failure |
|---|---|---|
| **MEL analytical composition** | **one universe** | `cross_universe` — a **category error**, not a disposition |
| **frame assembly** | **many universes, one anchor** | no failure; juxtaposition, with `(anchor, universe)` disclosed per series |
| **governed passage** | **between universes** | refuse, naming the absent passage |

The v7.1 acceptance suite and the joint-consistency case ledger contain **zero occurrences of "universe"**.

## 5. Determination closure and multidimensional composition

**The LAST/SUM correction is preserved, and the candidate rule is right.**

> **Family determination may close under composition only where universal analytical law establishes the required
> composition/interchange premises.**

**Grounding.** ToD §11.2 — *"**Nor does the witness-coherence theorem prove that SUM and LAST commute across
dimensions. An interchange law would need its own premises**, including the relevant participation and
alignment."* ToD §6.2 — composite coherence *"does not prove that arbitrary changes of analytical law commute."*
ToD §4.1 — *"A global LAST, a LAST taken separately for each customer, and a SUM of those customer results are
**not automatically one family.**"*

**The two failure modes stay distinct, and the distinction is load-bearing:**

| | what it is | disposition |
|---|---|---|
| **clause-level incoherence** | two **clauses** claim incompatible determinations of the same family at the same location | **a declaration defect** — the family is not a function |
| **path-level disagreement** | individually lawful clauses **do not compose** under the available premises | **silence** — no clause claimed the composite, so nothing was asserted about it |

**Without the distinction, adopting closure would break Balance outright**: a ragged ledger could not declare both
`SUM over {account}` and `LAST over {day}`. **A clause is a claim; a composition path is a derivation the engine
might attempt.**

### 5.1 Is rectangularity a family fact? — **No. It is derived, and from two places the model already has.**

The interchange premise for a **selector** paired with any reducer is that the participating domain be a
**rectangle** in the two axes — equivalently, that \(q:S\hookrightarrow A_1\times A_2\) **fill the product** over
the region concerned. Its two inputs:

| input | jurisdiction |
|---|---|
| the projection structure, and whether the image fills the product | **universe geometry** — Frame-QL §9.2's *"the image need not fill the Cartesian product"* is exactly this fact stated negatively |
| the participating domain \(D\) | **the resolved source** — §1.7 |

> **Neither is a family field, and no third input exists.** Rectangularity is therefore **entailed**, and v8
> should state it as an entailment obligation on the interchange law rather than as anything a family declares.

⚠ **And the refinement that matters:** since \(D\) is selected by *the law **and the resolved request*** (ToD
§4.2), **rectangularity can be request-dependent.** A universe may establish that the *eligible* region is
rectangular; a request that narrows participation can make it ragged. **An interchange licence must be checked
against the resolved \(D\), not only against the universe.**

## 7. Identity reconciliation — `identity_at` withdrawn

**Huayin's objection is correct and I accept it.** `identity_at(F,A)` was introduced to resolve a real tension,
but naming it a species of *identity* created a second family-identity object where none is needed.

### 7.1 The three terms, and the third is published

| term | what it is | published? |
|---|---|---|
| **family identity** \(F\) | what the declaration constitutes — the normalized resolved clause set (R1″) | ToD §2.2, §3.9 |
| **measure identity** \(F@A\) | *which quantity at which location*. **Measure = family @ anchor**, exactly as ToD has it | ToD, unchanged |
| **the resolved determination of \(F@A\)** | *how* that measure is determined — the derivation, with its operands resolved recursively | **ToD §3.7's analytical lineage records precisely this**, and §3.8 already separates it from sufficient-state dependency |

> **No new object. The third term is analytical lineage, which v7.1 already has**, and the only thing v8 adds is
> that **lineage is per-measure, not per-family** — which §3.7's own phrase *"constitutive ancestry"* supports,
> since ancestry at a coarse anchor and at the grounding anchor are not the same ancestry.

### 7.2 The theorem, in the smallest vocabulary

> **Adding a determination clause to \(F\) changes the family identity of \(F\). It does not change the resolved
> determination of \(F@A\) for any \(A\) whose determination that clause does not participate in.**
>
> **A construction depends on its operands' measure identities and on their resolved determinations — and on
> nothing else about the operand families.**

**Corollary, which is the theorem Huayin asked for, verbatim:** adding a clause for Balance elsewhere does not
change the identity of an existing construction over `balance@day`, **unless** the new clause changes the resolved
determination of `balance@day`. Adding `LAST over {day}` does not — Balance at `{account,day}` is still determined
by its grounding clause. Adding a **second grounding clause at the same anchor** does, **and then the construction
genuinely is a different thing**, which is correct.

**R1′ and R1″ are unaffected** and remain as stated. R1″ now reads over family identity, and the resolved
determination inherits normalization from it.

## 8. The material-measure boundary

### 8.1 The tuple is smaller than proposed

`(U, A, V)` has no \(F\) in it, and without \(F\) a material measure cannot distinguish `Revenue@{store,day}` from
`Cost@{store,day}`. Taking \(F\) as the identity of the material object:

> **\(U\) is derivable from \(F\)**, by the one-family-one-universe invariant (§1.3). **So the carried content is
> \((A, V)\), and \(U\) need not be stored at all.**

**That is the invariant paying rent.** It is also a falsification test: if a material measure genuinely needed to
carry \(U\) independently of \(F\), the invariant would be false.

### 8.2 `NA` — consistent, with one narrowing that matters

> **`NA` = the anchor point exists, and this measure is ineligible there.**

**It has a published home.** It is exactly Contract Calculus §15.4's third embedding case, \(a\in P\setminus E\),
materialized: \((0_\kappa,0,0)\) — contributing nothing to value **and not counted**. So a reducer meeting `NA`
must read it as a **domain fact, not a value**, which is precisely what CC's embedding does.

> ⚠ **The narrowing: `NA` is sound only where eligibility is *resolved and negative*.** `NA` is a **positive
> analytical claim**. Writing it where eligibility is merely *unresolved* manufactures a positive fact from
> silence — **the same error pattern as structural zero**, and the drift already found in the implementation
> corpus. **v8 should state this as a constraint on the material layer, not leave it to convention.**

### 8.3 The four values that are all "zero-ish", kept apart — and they are

| situation | material value | why |
|---|---|---|
| **ineligible** (Store A before opening) | **`NA`** | resolved-negative eligibility; CC's \(a\in P\setminus E\) |
| **eligible, positively known-empty contributing fiber, SUM** | **`0`** | the monoid identity, applied to a **known**-empty fiber. ToD §6.1: *"not a substitute for an unknown domain"* |
| **eligible, genuine zero contribution** | **`0`** | an ordinary established value |
| **eligible, required contribution unsupported** | **neither `NA` nor `0`** — no established entry | CC §15.5: *"no value is defined outside \(S'_{q,h}\), **even when the aggregate monoid has an identity**"* |

**Two lawful Revenue values both equal `0` and the material layer does not record why. That is correct** — the
distinction is in the *derivation*, not the value, and the derivation is external. **Discarding it is the right
kind of forgetting.**

### 8.4 The smallest irreducibly missing information

**Two states from §2's taxonomy cannot be represented, and both are the same kind of thing:**

| state | why the tuple cannot hold it |
|---|---|
| **unresolved existence** | whether the point is in \(\Omega_U\) is unsettled, so whether there *is* a cell is unsettled. The tuple can have a cell or not; it cannot have a *maybe* |
| **unresolved eligibility** | `NA` asserts ineligibility **positively**; an absent entry asserts nothing about eligibility. **Neither says "unsettled"** |

> **But neither is a value fact.** Both are **standing** facts, and the candidate already places standing outside
> the material measure alongside support. **So the answer is: \((A, V\in X\cup\{\mathsf{NA}\})\) is consistent with
> candidate v8 semantics, provided unresolved existence and unresolved eligibility are carried externally with
> support** — and provided the material layer never resolves an unsettled standing into `NA` for convenience.
>
> **Nothing is irreducibly missing from the value representation. What is missing is missing by design, and
> belongs where the candidate already puts it.**

### 8.5 Composite measures — the design direction is right, and generalizes

The direction *"MEAN should not be materialized as an independent material measure"* is **corroborated by
published text**: ToD §5.2 — *"For MEAN, a displayed scalar generally loses the weight required for exact
continuation. Its SUM and COUNT basis retains that information."*

> ⚡ **But "composite" is the wrong criterion, and the corpus supplies the right one.** ToD §11.3 is titled
> *"Displayed LAST values need not determine continuation"* — **LAST is not a composite and fails the same
> test**, because the scalar loses the witness.
>
> **The criterion is: a family may be materialized as a scalar exactly where its displayed value is a sufficient
> basis for its own admitted continuations.**
>
> | | scalar sufficient? | materialize as a scalar? |
> |---|---|---|
> | SUM, COUNT, MIN, MAX | **yes** — the value *is* the state | yes |
> | MEAN | **no** — needs \((\Sigma,N)\) | no; compute, or materialize the basis |
> | **LAST** | **no** — needs the witness (§11.3) | **no** — and this is the case the "composite" framing would have missed |
>
> This is a **derived** criterion, not a preference, and it is strictly better than the composite/non-composite
> split. **A scalar may still be materialized as an answer; what it may not be is a basis.** ToD §10.7: *"A stored
> answer and retained sufficient state are different claims."*

**And the tuple survives this.** Materializing `SUM@A` as `(A, V)` is sound; materializing `MEAN@A` as `(A, V)`
is sound *as an answer* and unsound *as a basis* — a distinction that lives in the certificate, not in the value.
