## A.9 R1′ — single authority

**Subject.** What happens when an author states something the declaration plus universal law already entails.

> **A derived fact gets its authority from entailment. Restating it does not create another authority.**

**What it settles.** The principle was previously carried in a stronger form — *redundant-but-agreeing
declarations are ill-formed* — which is **not entailed by the theory**. Rejecting a restatement, checking it, and
ignoring it all preserve single authority; the choice among them is an **authoring-surface** decision, not an
ontological one.

**Why the stronger form was withdrawn.** It made **family well-formedness non-monotone under universe
extension**: where a world admitted one governed order, stating it was forbidden, so every such declaration
omitted it — and adding a second governed order to the universe then invalidated family declarations whose
quantities had not changed. Each forced edit is churn the succession machinery must distinguish from a real
change of definition.

**The rule that replaces it.** The operative question is not *is this derived?* but ***is this uniquely
entailed?*** A field is **always permitted**, **required** exactly where its fact is not uniquely entailed, and
**checked** where it is, with disagreement refused. This distinguishes **restating** from **resolving an
ambiguity** — two different acts the stronger form had conflated.

**What it does not do.** It does not weaken minimal constitution. Exactly the same facts are constitutive and
exactly the same facts derived.

## A.10 R1″ — identity over resolved semantics

> **Declarations resolving to the same semantic constitution identify the same family, regardless of redundant
> spelling.**

**Why it is the load-bearing half.** This is what A.9's stronger form was really protecting. If identity were
computed over authored text, a restatement genuinely *would* change \(\Sigma(F)\) — but the correct repair is to
canonicalize identity, not to forbid the utterance, because text-based identity makes **one quantity into two
depending on verbosity**.

**Consequences.**

- Identity is computed over the **normalized resolved clause set**: resolved law and identity-bearing parameters,
  constitutive operand anchors and roles, governed world references **by identity**, normalized scope, resolved
  eligibility, and the grounding handle.
- **Out:** spelling, clause order, redundant-but-agreeing statements, aliases, the family's **name**, and every
  consequence (extension, empty-fiber outcome, sufficient-state basis, result domain, lineage, argument shape).
- **You cannot mint a quantity by renaming one**, and two identically-resolved constructions are one family
  under two names — which is canonicalization, arriving as a consequence rather than a mechanism.
- **The grounding handle is the one non-normalizable identity input.** A textual edit to it is conservatively a
  re-ratification. Known, and now precisely located.

## A.11 Retirement of contribution multiplicity as a primitive family fact

> **If multiplicity is governed, the index over which it exists must itself be governed — which is
> individuation, or another governed construction. It does not belong hidden inside an observational family
> clause.**

**The argument.** A multiplicity greater than one at a point is **a reduction over an index**. An index the
universe does not individuate **cannot be governed** — one cannot count what has no governed identity, and two
contributions distinguishable by nothing are not two. So a *governed* multiplicity requires a governed index,
and a governed index **is** individuation. The test *"same universe, differing only by governed multiplicity"*
therefore cannot be satisfied: **there is no smallest example, because there is no example.**

**Where the apparent cases go.** Distinguishable contributions → **universe individuation**, after which an
ordinary fiber-reducing clause carries the rest — **with a scope, which the multiplicity field never had**. Two
independent observations at one point → **two grounding clauses** with the existing agreement obligation, which
is what multiplicity was most often mistaken for. Several values that are one thing → **the value type**. A
payment settling three invoices → **relationship geometry**: a cover, not a partition, and an **allocation
construction** if a number is wanted. N carrier rows at the family's grain → **realization conformance**.
Contributions indistinguishable even in principle → **not governed at all**.

**The published consequence.** Ruling 9's antecedent — *"analytical where contributions genuinely coincide"* — is
not false. **It is empty.**

## A.12 Governed point selection versus population-changing restriction

Two different semantic acts that request syntax spells identically.

| | act | example |
|---|---|---|
| **coordinate pin** | selects governed analytical points. **No new population; identity preserved** | `WHERE store = 'SEA'` — this is the family at `{store, week}`, at one coordinate |
| **value predicate** | may **define a different participating population** — a different quantity | `WHERE revenue > 0` — the predicate ranges over a *value*, not a coordinate |

> **A value predicate cannot acquire constitutive authority merely because request syntax can express it.**

**Where the second belongs.** By exact analogy with a governed placement: **constitute a governed classification
as a constituent**, after which the predicate is a coordinate and the restriction is a pin. The cost falls in the
right place — *"carrying revenue"* is a business judgement (is zero the same as absent? is a full refund
revenue-bearing?) and should not be inferable from a `WHERE`. Where nobody will constitute it, the disposition is
**Clarify**.

**Two neighbours kept distinct, each with a decidable tell.**

- **Definedness is contagious, not selective.** A fiber `{10, 20, undefined}` is **undefined** under definedness
  and **15** under a population that excludes undefined points. Definedness *shrinks the law's domain*; a
  population *is* the law's domain.
- **Support is not a population.** *"The mean over the week's eligible orders, partially supported"* and *"the
  mean over the orders we have data for"* **return the same number** and are different analytical objects. The
  test: **if the value can change while the world, the declaration and the request all stay fixed, the population
  is support-based and the thing is not a quantity.**

**Qualification, deliberately not generalized.** This is stated for the cases tested and **not as a universal
Frame-QL theorem**. It is not yet established for predicates over derived constituents whose governance is itself
in question, for formation-sensitive predicates that pin for one clause and subset for another within a single
request, or for contextual restrictions interacting with co-participation.

## A.13 The determination-clause model — `source × scope`

> **A family is a name bound to determination clauses. A determination clause is `source × scope`.**

**Source — two kinds, with a closure argument rather than an enumeration.** A determination either appeals to
something **outside** the formal system (the governed world) or derives from something **inside** it (a cited
universal law applied to measures). There is no third place a value can come from; every apparent third is a
**naming**, **universe**, or **realization** act. A governed constant is an observation of a governed artifact; an
alias is naming; an imputation is a law and therefore its own family; a cache is realization.

**Scope — a predicate on a governed projection**, decidable from geometry alone, answering *where this clause
determines the family's value*. It is **per clause**; the extension is **per family** and derived. It must not
become an enumerated \(\mathcal A_F\), a B-anchor, \(P_F\), an execution capability, an evidence predicate, or a
serving policy, and each exclusion has a one-line invariance test — notably: **scope's truth value must be
invariant under changing which data is loaded**, and **the add-a-clause test**, under which scope *grows* a
family's extension where a prohibition set could only be *shrunk*, at a different index.

**The separation, as a signature rather than a rule.** Determination resolves as

```
determines(F, A) ⟺ ∃c ∈ Clauses(F). scope(c)(I_c ⪰ A) ∧ ∀operands ⟨H, J⟩. determines(H, J)
```

— and the recursive call is **a boolean at one location**. Nothing can reach `Clauses(H)` except through it.
**A construction consumes an operand measure; it cannot consume or inherit the operand family's determination
clauses, because the resolution function has no term for them.**

**One condition is newly required, and it is the positive successor to the B-anchor.** A family must be a
**function**: two clauses that both apply at a location must not provably disagree. Declaring `LAST over {day}`
alongside `SUM over {day}` is refused **at declaration time** because Balance would not be a function; declaring
only `SUM over {day}` is coherent, and it is not the theory's place to forbid an unusual business. **The
prohibition was never needed. Functionality was.**

**And multiple applicable clauses are not automatically malformed** — three distinct situations, discriminated by
whether disagreement is decidable before data: **co-determination** (redundancy; normalize), **alternative
derivation with an agreement obligation** (fine at constitution; disagreement is an *evidence* disposition), and
**provable disagreement** (a declaration defect). **Ambiguity lives in the request; incoherence lives in the
declaration**, and `Clarify` is only ever for the first.

## A.14 A family's domain is derived, not authored

> **A family's "domain" is where its determination clauses have meaning. It is not an independently authored
> family object.**

```
𝒜_F  =  { A : determines(F, A) }            NOTATION for a derived predicate
```

**What follows.**

- **No per-anchor object could have been right**, even for observational families: with a partial value type,
  definedness is **below anchor granularity** — a family stands at `{region}` while particular region-points are
  undefined.
- **Adding a clause widens the domain monotonically** and changes no value already determined. Under the old
  negative encoding, the same business act would have required *shrinking a prohibition set* — a different edit,
  at a different index, which is the clearest sign the index was wrong.
- **A declaration is either complete, and its domain derived, or incomplete, and refused.** There is no third
  state, so no status is needed to record one.
- **The notation survives; the object does not.** Eliminating an authored family domain does not require
  eliminating the mathematical fact that a partial law has a domain — but nothing may **consult** the extension
  while deciding a construction over the family's measures. That check is the defect, under whatever name it
  travels.

## V. Evidence for the v7.2-versus-v8.0 decision

**Flagged, not decided.** The evidence divides cleanly, and the division is itself the finding: **the theory is
continuous; the ontology and the declaration surface are not.**

### V.1 Evidence consistent with a point release (v7.2)

- **No published statement is contradicted.** §4.1's *"\(\mathcal A_F\) is not a proposed registry or new
  object"* is **vindicated**; §7.2 on order resolution is **confirmed**; §11.5.1's canonicalization is
  **confirmed and leaned on**; §14.5 and §3.7 survive intact and load-bearing.
- **What is withdrawn is mostly unpublished** — development-journal entries A.1 and A.2, and their
  constructed-family-propagation question, which **dissolves rather than resolves**.
- **What is added is mostly filler for gaps the published text already left open** — §5.2's *"admitted
  refinement"* is answered (*admitted by a clause's scope*), and §7.2's order question is closed in the direction
  the text already pointed.
- Several conclusions are **recoveries of Version 6.1**, not novelties.

### V.2 Evidence pointing to a major release (v8.0)

- **Objects leave the ontology**: \(P_F\), the family root, C3 family-domain standing, `formation.kind`, the
  `domain` and `movement` declaration fields, `CONSTRUCTED_DOMAIN_UNDECIDED`, the B-anchor, the stock/flow
  taxonomy, and now **contribution multiplicity**. A point release that removes nine ontological commitments is
  doing a major release's work.
- **Identity semantics change.** R1″ changes **what counts as the same family**. That is not additive: two
  declarations previously distinct may now be one, and any downstream identity computed over authored text is
  invalidated.
- **The declaration surface narrows.** Existing declarations say things the model cannot express — every
  `BLOCKED` set, every `participation` string, every `prohibited_constituents` list. A narrowing is a breaking
  change even when every *quantity* survives.
- **A new well-formedness condition is imposed** — coherence — which can refuse declarations that were
  previously admitted.
- **One question is closed that the published text left open** — request-local restriction (A.12) — which is a
  theory *addition*, not a clarification.

### V.3 The axis the decision actually runs along

> **Does the version number track the continuity of the *theory*, or the compatibility of the *declaration*?**

On the first reading this is **v7.2**: nothing published is overturned, and the changes are withdrawals of
development hypotheses plus fillers for acknowledged gaps. On the second it is **v8.0**: the ontology loses nine
objects, identity semantics change, and the surface narrows.

**A third option worth naming, and not recommended here:** separate the two — a **v7.2 of the theory** carrying
A.9–A.14, and a **v1.0 of a distinct declaration contract** that the theory does not version. This would stop the
declaration's compatibility story from driving the theory's version number, at the cost of a second versioned
artifact. **Recorded as an option, not a recommendation.**

**No decision is taken here.**
