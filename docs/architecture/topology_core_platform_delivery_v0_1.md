# Columna Core / Platform / Cloud Topology and Supersession Record

**Project:** Columna / datumwise  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 28 August 2026  
**Status:** Current internal architecture ruling  
**Scope:** Naming and relationship among Columna, Columna Core, Columna Platform, Columna Cloud, Studio, and private/OEM delivery forms

---

## 0. Purpose

This record freezes the current topology of Columna after several earlier documents used **Core**, **Platform**, **Cloud**, **Enterprise**, **Pro**, **Studio**, and related names on overlapping axes.

The governing distinction is now:

> **Core and Platform name architectures.**  
> **Cloud, self-hosted, private enterprise, and OEM name delivery forms.**

Architecture answers:

> What kind of runtime and authority structure is this?

Delivery answers:

> Who operates it, where does it run, and under what service or commercial arrangement?

The two axes may combine. They must not be used to redefine one another.

This is an internal architecture and supersession record. It does not announce a commercial product, pricing plan, hosted service, enterprise tier, or release schedule.

---

## 1. The public identity of Columna

The public description remains deliberately simple:

> **Columna is infrastructure for governed analytical service.**

Columna is the umbrella executable system emerging from the datumwise research program.

It makes governed analytical meaning, requests, adjudication, realization, and serving executable. The research does not depend on Columna; Columna is accountable to the research.

Core, Platform, Cloud, Studio, and OEM should not each acquire an independent market positioning. They are parts of the architecture-and-delivery topology beneath the one public description.

## 2. The two-axis topology

```text
ARCHITECTURE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Columna Core                         Columna Platform
conventional realization            identity-centered,
of governed meaning                  placement-independent
                                     governed realization


DELIVERY
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
self-hosted     managed Cloud     private enterprise     OEM / embedded
```

The axes combine rather than form a ladder.

Possible combinations include:

- self-hosted Core;
- a managed service initially using Core-style realization;
- self-hosted or private Platform;
- managed Platform Cloud;
- Platform capabilities embedded in another product;
- governed kernels or interfaces delivered through OEM arrangements.

A delivery form does not acquire Platform standing merely because it is hosted, managed, large, multi-tenant, or sold to an enterprise.

An architecture does not become Cloud merely because it can be operated remotely.

## 3. Columna Core

### Definition

> **Columna Core compiles governed meaning into conventional execution infrastructure.**

Core is the open, self-service architecture that proves governed analytical meaning and serving authority can be made explicit while conventional databases, connectors, execution engines, and compute substrates perform material work.

### Core may include

- governed Manifold authoring and publication;
- Studio;
- multiple Manifolds;
- Frame-QL;
- planning and adjudication;
- certification and positive serving admission;
- governed serving through API, MCP, UI, or agent surfaces;
- multiple physical sources where they can be faithfully governed within the Core realization;
- source connectors;
- local or backend execution;
- private realization mappings;
- Core-private execution images;
- caches and materializations that remain subordinate to governed identity and law.

### Core does not need in order to be complete

Core does not need to own a fully backend-independent identity/state runtime.

It does not need to compose governed measure state across independent execution domains under Columna-owned identity.

It does not need to become a hosted service.

It does not need to be limited to one source, one Manifold, one user, or a small deployment.

### Core question

Core asks:

> **Can this governed data world be realized and served faithfully here?**

Core is not a deliberately weakened precursor whose useful semantic capability is withheld to create a paid tier.

---

## 4. Columna Platform

### Definition

> **Columna Platform preserves one governed Data World while material realization moves across execution placements and independent execution domains.**

The earlier compact definition remains valid:

> **Columna Platform preserves governed analytical identity independently of any one execution environment.**

The newer formulation makes the material-placement consequence explicit:

> **Execution placement may change; governed identity, law, support, conditions, and standing may not.**

### Platform begins when Columna owns

- canonical governed identity across independent realization domains;
- anchor and universe identity across those domains;
- sufficient-state composition;
- support and typed absence;
- freshness, provenance, and certification state;
- identity-keyed materialization;
- lawful cross-domain composition;
- alignment and reconciliation where identities do not already coincide;
- governed return that preserves the standing and conditions of the result.

### Material realization may move among

- verified source-side pushdown;
- connector-mediated source extraction at governed grain;
- local Arrow or DuckDB-style compute;
- identity-keyed materialized measure state;
- embedded governed kernels;
- customer-controlled compute;
- future open native formats or catalogs;
- future native custody.

No single placement defines Platform.

### Permanent invariant

> **Two physical runtimes are acceptable. Two meanings of a measure are not.**

### Platform question

Platform asks:

> **Can governed data identity remain stable and lawfully composable while realization moves across the organization?**

Platform is genuinely new architecture. It is not hosted Core plus adapters.

## 5. Columna Cloud

### Definition

> **Columna Cloud is a managed delivery form of Columna capabilities.**

Cloud names who operates the service and how it is delivered. It does not name a third semantic or runtime architecture.

### Cloud may deliver

- Core-style governed realization;
- selected Platform capabilities;
- a fully managed Platform when those capabilities actually exist;
- managed authoring and publication lifecycle;
- managed registry, certification, monitoring, materialization, and serving;
- enterprise networking, isolation, operations, and support.

### Cloud must state what it actually exhibits

Hostedness does not establish:

- backend-independent identity;
- cross-domain sufficient-state composition;
- Platform custody;
- identity-keyed materialization;
- governed portability across execution domains.

A managed service should claim only the Core or Platform properties it actually demonstrates.

### Cloud rule

> **Cloud is not a third architecture. Hostedness does not define Platform.**

A managed Core-style service can exist.

A private or customer-operated Platform can exist.

A managed Platform Cloud can exist.

These are valid combinations of the two axes.

### Current standing

This record does not claim that Columna Cloud currently exists as a released or purchasable service.

“Cloud” remains a delivery name available for a future managed offering. Any public use must follow the honest-hatching rule and distinguish shipped service from direction.

---

## 6. Private enterprise and OEM / embedded

### Private enterprise

Private enterprise names a delivery and operating arrangement in which Columna runs under customer control or within customer infrastructure.

It may use Core or Platform architecture.

Its differentiators may include:

- private networking;
- custody;
- organizational identity and lifecycle services;
- enterprise reliability and assurance;
- managed certification and monitoring;
- customer-controlled materialization and state;
- regulated deployment requirements.

“Enterprise” must not be used as a synonym for Platform.

### OEM / embedded

OEM or embedded names an integration form in which Columna laws, request interfaces, adjudication, kernels, or serving behavior are incorporated into another system.

It may range from:

- embedded request/adjudication interfaces;
- certification in another product’s lifecycle;
- governed execution behind another vendor’s agent surface;
- extension kernels in foreign engines;
- later identity/state infrastructure supplied as a component.

OEM is not a separate data ontology and not a third runtime architecture.

## 7. Shared Columna capabilities

Several capabilities belong to the shared Columna spine. They must not be used to define commercial or architectural tiers.

### Manifold

A Manifold is the governed ontology of a particular enterprise’s or domain’s data under the Theory of Data.

The authored and published Manifold belongs to the Data World.

Material bindings belong to realization.

### Studio

Studio is the human authoring and ratification surface for governed Manifolds.

It belongs to the shared Columna architecture and therefore to Core as a complete self-service system. It is not a separate “Pro” or Platform-only capability.

Studio authors the declared world. It does not author the execution image.

### Frame-QL

Frame-QL is a ToD-native declarative and representational language of the Data World.

It is shared across Core and Platform.

It does not define either architecture and is not a premium tier.

### Manifold lifecycle and registry

Draft, resolution, accountable ratification, immutable publication, version selection, and publication identity are shared concerns.

Platform may provide a broader organization-wide implementation, but the lifecycle itself is not Platform-exclusive.

### Governed serving surfaces

MCP, API, UI, agent, and other requester surfaces consume the governed serving contract.

They should not need different analytical meanings merely because Core or Platform realizes the request.

### Multi-Manifold capability

Multi-Manifold authoring and serving are shared Columna requirements.

The number of Manifolds or physical sources does not define the Core/Platform boundary.

## 8. Shared architectural spine

```text
THEORY OF DATA / DATA LAW
          ↓
AUTHORED MANIFOLD
          ↓
ACCOUNTABLE RATIFICATION
          ↓
GOVERNED PUBLICATION
          ↓
FRAME-QL / GOVERNED REQUEST
          ↓
PLANNING / ADJUDICATION
          ↓
GOVERNED SERVING CONTRACT
          ↓
MCP / API / UI / AGENTS
          │
   execution-provider seam
      _____│_____
     │           │
   CORE       PLATFORM
```

On the Core side:

```text
governed publication
        +
private realization mapping
        ↓
compiler / lowering
        ↓
Core-private execution image
        ↓
planner-owned lawful operation
        ↓
conventional material execution
```

On the Platform side:

```text
governed publication / identity
        ↓
governed identity and state runtime
        ↓
lawful realization-placement choice
        ↓
source pushdown / local compute /
materialized state / embedded kernel /
future native custody
        ↓
governed return
```

The Platform owns the realization contract. It does not need to own every engine that performs material work.

## 9. Permanent invariants

The following survive architecture and delivery choices.

### Meaning before realization

> **Meaning must exist before realization. Mapping realizes meaning; it does not create it.**

### No law loss in lowering

> **Lowering may reduce coverage. It must never reduce law.**

If a realization cannot preserve required law, it refuses.

### Substrate does not acquire meaning

> **The substrate performs computation. It does not decide what computation means.**

### Positive authority

> **Declaration is not certification. Absence of contradiction is not permission. Positive admission authorizes execution.**

### Planner / engine separation

> **The planner chooses the lawful operation. The engine performs it.**

### Publishing and ratification

> **Publishing consumes ratification. It does not mint authority.**

AI and software may assist authoring. Accountable ratification supplies authority.

### Runtime plurality

> **Two physical runtimes are acceptable. Two meanings of a measure are not.**

### Cloud honesty

> **Hostedness does not establish Platform. Cloud claims only the capabilities it exhibits.**

### Platform identity

> **Execution placement may change; governed identity, law, support, conditions, and standing may not.**

## 10. Commercial and delivery value

The architecture does not prescribe a commercial model.

Legitimate paid value may center on:

- managed hosting;
- operational assurance;
- custody;
- identity and state infrastructure;
- private deployment;
- enterprise networking;
- lifecycle operations;
- monitoring, freshness, certification, and audit;
- managed materialization;
- cross-domain reconciliation;
- support and service levels;
- OEM integration;
- authoring workflow and operational comfort.

Paid value should not depend on artificially removing basic analytical law, Frame-QL semantics, multi-Manifold capability, or useful self-service execution from Core.

A useful historic maxim remains:

> **Open laws and open formats; paid custody, assurance, and comfort.**

This is commercial direction, not a currently announced offer.

## 11. Supersession ledger

| Superseded interpretation | Current ruling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Core = local or small; Platform = cloud or enterprise** | Core and Platform are architectures; local/cloud/enterprise are delivery and scale properties | Hosting and size do not create identity-centered architecture |
| **Core = one source; Platform = many sources** | Core may use multiple sources; Platform begins with Columna-owned identity and composition across independent execution domains | Source count does not settle authority or identity |
| **Platform = hosted Core plus adapters** | Platform is a governed identity/state architecture | Adapters and hosting alone do not preserve identity across domains |
| **Cloud = Platform** | Cloud is a managed delivery form that may deliver Core or Platform capabilities | Architecture and operation are separate axes |
| **Platform = paid tier; Core = free tier** | Architecture is independent of licensing and pricing | Commercial packaging must not define semantic/runtime architecture |
| **Studio is a separate Pro or enterprise tier** | Studio is a shared authoring and ratification surface, included in complete Core | Authoring meaning is part of the governed lifecycle |
| **Multi-Manifold belongs only to Platform** | Multi-Manifold is a shared Columna requirement | Number of governed domains is not the architectural boundary |
| **Hosted Platform is necessarily a separate third category** | Use managed Platform, private Platform, or Platform delivered through Cloud as needed | Avoid multiplying architecture names for delivery combinations |
| **Pro / Enterprise / Future define the system architecture** | Such names may later describe products or packages only | Product packaging must remain accountable to the architecture |
| **DuckDB, Polars, local compute, or pushdown defines the architecture** | Execution placement is policy below Data World law | The governed object must survive all verified placements |
| **The Metric Engine must remain a permanent separate layer** | Mechanical compute may thin, move, or disappear as a compensating layer while governed law remains explicit | Engine shape is not Columna identity |
| **AtomDB is the current Platform plan** | Native identity-oriented custody is a long-horizon option | Near-term Platform must be gated by evidence, not destination mythology |
| **Member / atom terminology defines future storage** | Current Data World identity and measure-family vocabulary govern new work | Older visions are idea lineage, not current naming authority |

## 16. Compact ruling

> **Columna Core** is the complete open/self-service architecture that compiles governed Data World meaning into conventional material execution.
>
> **Columna Platform** is the identity-centered architecture that keeps the governed Data World invariant while material realization moves across execution placements and independent domains.
>
> **Columna Cloud** is a managed delivery of Columna capabilities. Hostedness does not define Platform, and Cloud may claim only the Core or Platform capabilities it actually exhibits.
>
> **Private enterprise and OEM** are additional delivery forms, not additional semantic architectures.
>
> **Studio, Manifold lifecycle, Frame-QL, multi-Manifold capability, and governed serving surfaces are shared.**

## 17. Standing statements required at adoption

*Sections 0–16 are the record as ruled. This section was added when the record was
committed to the repository, to state explicitly the facts the ruling requires this
repository to hold as current. It carries the same authority as the rest of the record.*

### 17.1 Architecture is not a package

**Columna Core is an architecture.** `columna-core` is **one runtime package**, not the
complete Core system. Complete Core, as §§3 and 7 define it, additionally requires the
shared authoring, ratification, and lifecycle surfaces. A statement true of the
architecture is not thereby true of the package, and a statement true of the package is
not thereby true of the architecture.

**Columna Platform is an identity-centered architecture.** It is not hosted Core, not
Core plus adapters, and not a commercial tier. **Cloud, self-hosted, private enterprise,
managed service, and OEM are delivery or operational forms.** Cloud is not a third
architecture, and hostedness does not create Platform.

### 17.2 Architectural belonging is not shipped implementation

> **Architectural belonging must never be described as shipped implementation.**

Studio, governed authoring, the Manifold lifecycle, and the multi-Manifold lifecycle
**belong to the shared/Core architecture**. That is a statement about where they belong,
not about what exists today.

**The current public package set does not exhibit complete governed self-service
authoring.** The author → ratify → publish third of the governed lifecycle is not present
in this repository. It is reachable only through private producer checkouts, and OF-28
(`specs/open_forks.md`) remains an open stop-gate against opening a public governed
authoring surface. Any document that describes governed authoring as available to a user
of the published packages is false as of this record.

### 17.3 Which artifact is the governed evidence

**Firstlight is governed machine evidence.** The runtime unit under
`packages/columna-server/src/columna_server/governed/firstlight/` is the repository's
governed proof: a publication, a compiled execution image, and a lowering receipt that
binds them, admitted as `ENTRY_GOVERNED` and exercised end to end by standing tests.

**The default public Cascadia quickstart remains a legacy path and must be typed
honestly.** `demo/cascadia` is hand-authored, carries no `SOURCE_MANIFOLD`, and is
classified `ENTRY_LEGACY` by admission. It demonstrates serving, disclosure, and the four
moods. It does **not** demonstrate the governed path, and no document may present it as
though it does.

### 17.4 No product claims

No product claim may be created for Cloud, private enterprise, OEM, managed Platform, or
hosted Platform. None of these presently has a factual product referent. Where a
capability is architecturally shared but unbuilt, it is to be typed **ROADMAP**; where it
is a delivery idea, **DELIVERY-OPERATIONS**; where it named a commercial tier that does
not exist, **RETIRED**.
